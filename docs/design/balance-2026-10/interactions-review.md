# Rules interaction review

These are concrete desk checks of the agreed design, not changes to its rules. **Confirmed** means the conclusion follows from stated rules or exact calculation. **Unresolved** means a necessary rule or card detail is missing. **Conditional risk** means an illustrative card combination could cause a problem if the final catalog permits it.

## Highest priority interactions

### 1 Healing access determines lasting pressure

**Model finding.** Repeated full healing need not make ordinary fights longer, but it can almost eliminate wounds in a fixture that wounds an attack-only party frequently. This is a stronger effect than a small damage adjustment. Keep the agreed repeatable healing while testing access, reach, initiative, action timing, and enemy pressure. Potion price, availability, and recipe time are part of this balance, not merely equipment flavor.

Do not respond by silently adding once-per-combat limits; Bruce explicitly rejected that direction. Decide what a typical encounter should cost a prepared party and what happens to a party without a dedicated healer. Record wounds, not just victory and remaining HP.

**Enemy-side extension:** testing only plain attackers was insufficient. Hypothetical repeatable NPC healing produces stalls in the direct-attack model. Two full-healing supporters can keep four HP-12 creatures alive beyond the 80-round cutoff even when targeted first. A self-healing HP-48 solo can restore at least 24 HP per Main, more than four Strong Novice attacks can remove through Resistance 1 (20 damage). Both full and half healing therefore deserve explicit enemy budgets and counterplay. Control, AoE, free attacks, and critical riders are absent from these experiments, so they do not prove such a creature unbeatable by a complete party. They do prove that its healing cannot be valued as an incidental trait.

### 2 A basic attack can lose all damage sensitivity to its roll

**Confirmed.** Basic damage 1/2/4 against Physical Resistance 3 becomes 1/1/1. Attribute, Boons, Banes, and outcome tier no longer change its ordinary damage, though critical effects, item properties, and other riders can still matter. A novice 2/4/6 attack against Resistance 3 becomes 1/1/3. Against Resistance 5 it is also 1/1/1.

This is not an argument to make basic attacks equal purchased attacks; their opportunity/free-attack role was intentional. It is a warning that high Resistance should be tested against that role. Raising the cap to 6 by Master tier does not automatically make Resistance 6 appropriate on every creature.

### 3 A flaming weapon does not always add one actual damage

**Confirmed.** Suppose a Weak basic attack deals one physical plus one fire damage into Physical Resistance 2. Physical becomes zero; fire remains one; total damage is one. Without the flame property, the attack would already inflict the minimum one. The property added a damage component but no additional HP loss.

Against Physical Resistance 4, every basic outcome is fully resisted physically, so a +1 fire property still leaves total damage at one for every outcome. This follows the agreed **minimum per event**, not per type. Write an example on the damage reference so players do not incorrectly add the bonus after the minimum. Decide whether that behavior matches the desired feel of special items before adjusting numbers.

### 4 Control has guaranteed contributions even under severe disadvantage

**Confirmed, for the agreed Grapple outcomes.** Each eligible grapple contributes at least one level. At attribute 0 with no Boons/Banes, two independent grapples reach Grappled 3 with probability 93.28%; three do so with certainty, excluding intervening escape or other effects. Even at three net Banes, two do so 38.92% of the time and three still suffice.

The grapplers pay their own action restrictions, so this is not free control. Nevertheless, two or three inexpensive bodies can trade their actions for a powerful solo creature's turn, with no self-release at Grappled 3. A strong solo's defense Banes do not prevent minimum contribution. Test grappler size, physical plausibility, timing, and explicit solo answers before assuming boss HP solves this. No blanket immunity or new size gate has been adopted.

### 5 Dazed may be more valuable than its damage trade suggests

**Conditional risk.** Three legal Dazed 1 applications remove Main Action, Maneuver, and Reaction until the condition ends. Movement remains available. If cheap attacks can repeatedly apply Dazed at Weak while still dealing damage, modest damage sacrifices can buy near-guaranteed control. It does not impose the grappler's reciprocal restrictions.

Test actual application tiers, durations, and removal methods together. Do not assign the same damage discount to Dazed 1 and an incidental movement rider without measuring the denied actions. This study does not invent a Dazed card or assume one exists in every party.

### 6 Unlimited summons have value beyond their commands

**Conditional risk with a confirmed rules interaction.** Uncommanded creatures still occupy squares, and ordinary creatures cannot move through occupied squares unless the occupant is prone. Unlimited persistent summons can form walls, obstruct approaches, occupy space, and absorb attacks even when only two receive commands. The command limit alone therefore does not cap their tactical value.

A second concern is the initial free Main command. If a summon attacks as well as the controller's ordinary attack and also provides a fresh body, summoning may outperform direct attacks every turn. This depends on its actual HP, damage, duration, and placement. Keep the agreed unlimited-summon policy for now; price and test complete summon cards rather than treating uncommanded summons as having zero value.

### 7 Free attacks scale through other characters and item effects

**Conditional risk.** The no-chaining rule prevents recursion; it does not cap the number of initial free attacks. A card granting three basic attacks can exceed one straightforward Novice attack even before special item riders. Those basic attacks can come from the party's best attribute and equipment rather than the granting character.

Cards granting attacks need an explicit number of eligible recipients/targets, timing, and target restrictions. Their cost includes every granted attack and rider. Keep this as a catalog budget check rather than replacing the agreed unrestricted frequency of legitimately granted free attacks.

### 8 Area attacks still have an unresolved target-specific roll rule

**Unresolved.** A player rolls once for an area attack, but targets may impose different Banes through defense or cover. One target behind heavy cover and another in the open cannot both be assigned the same difficulty without a deliberate rule. The numerical model contains no AoE and does not silently choose a resolution.

This must be settled before testing area damage budgets. The effect's shape and exclusions also determine whether allies, summons, and objects amplify or constrain its value.

### 9 Permanent wounds create a substantial lifetime death clock

**Confirmed.** Terminal wound odds are 0.46% on the first wound, 1.62% on the second, then 3.55% per wound at the five-die cap. The chance of at least one terminal result by ten wounds is 26.66%; by twenty, 48.90%. These figures exclude death marks and other final-death effects.

This can serve the intended dark attrition. It needs an explicit campaign expectation: if typical fights frequently produce one or more wounds, careers can become short even while the party rarely loses a battle. Relieving active wound penalties does not lower the permanent risk. Do not infer safety from a combat victory percentage.

### 10 Fortitude has a much larger death-roll effect than its HP contribution

**Confirmed for isolated Dying, no rescue, no Grit, no damage between rolls.** The eventual chance of reaching Last Words through death marks is 89.73% at Fortitude −2, 40.51% at 0, and 2.32% at +2. Standard results prolong the process; they do not stabilize. Wound-triggered terminal results are excluded from these percentages.

That dramatic spread may be intentional. Explain it during creation: a negative Fortitude is not merely one or two fewer HP. Actual play with allies will differ greatly because universal stabilization has no roll and healing is repeatable.

### 11 Stabilization and repeated damage can create wounds without much Grit use

**Confirmed.** A character at 0 is stabilized. A later damaging event puts them back into Dying, causing another permanent wound. An ally stabilizes them again, and the cycle can repeat. Spending no Grit does not prevent wound accumulation.

This rewards removing the threat or actually restoring HP rather than repeatedly stabilizing someone in danger. Make the consequence explicit on Stabilize. The simulations show campaigns accumulating wounds while much of the party's Grit remains unused; wounds and Grit are separate attrition measures, not interchangeable counters.

## Condition and timing checks

### 12 Several end-of-turn damage conditions need a clear event order

**Unresolved.** At 1 HP, Burning, Freezing, and Bleeding may drop a character to 0 and then add death marks through subsequent damage events. We agreed these effects resolve at turn end, but not who orders simultaneous effects or whether each condition is a separate event for death marks.

This can determine Last Words. Specify ordering and event boundaries once in the core timing rule; do not leave a dying character's fate to accidental paragraph order.

### 13 Resistance cannot fully stop damaging conditions under the minimum rule

**Confirmed if each condition tick is its own damaging event.** Burning 1 against Fire Resistance 6 still deals one. Likewise Freezing. Bleeding explicitly bypasses Resistance. Resistance never removes the condition itself.

This makes even small ongoing conditions meaningful, but accumulating different damage conditions may outperform increasing one rating because each event has its own floor. Verify that interpretation when settling event boundaries; do not quietly invent immunity.

### 14 Poison changed role and leaves old resistance assumptions behind

**Confirmed.** Poisoned is now a roll penalty, not periodic poison damage. Numeric Poison Resistance reduces poison damage; it does not automatically reduce Poisoned ratings. An antidote or ability must specify condition removal. Distinguish poisonous attacks that deal damage from the Poisoned condition in creature and equipment writing.

The same distinction matters for healing: restoring HP does not clear Poisoned, Dazed, or a wound. A Strong healing benefit must say what it removes or reduces.

### 15 Damage bypasses the escalating waking cost of Sleep

**Confirmed.** Asleep 3 takes three waking Main Actions, but damage wakes the character immediately. A healthy sleeping ally can be struck with a basic unarmed attack instead. It costs one Main Action and inflicts 1/2/4 damage; a later breath restores that HP.

This may be an acceptable emergency trade-off, but it weakens Sleep's rating as a measure of waking difficulty. Low-HP sleepers risk Dying and a wound, so the shortcut is not universally safe. Decide whether the distinction is desired rather than silently banning friendly attacks.

### 16 Condition ratings can exceed the effective roll penalty

**Confirmed.** Poisoned 5 can still cancel five Boons before the net cap, even though an unopposed roll uses only three Banes. A rating beyond three can also require more reduction to clear. Thus ratings beyond the dice cap are not meaningless, but players must track them even when the immediate roll looks unchanged.

Specify sensible application/removal values in cards. No global cap for every rated condition has been agreed, and imposing one here would undo the design.

### 17 Mixed fear sources and mixed removal rules need examples

**Unresolved.** Frightened depends on perceiving its source, but multiple applications merge ratings and retain the later expiry. If one source leaves and another stays, does the entire merged rating apply? Similarly, adding one supernatural Burning rating preserves its special removal restriction across the merged condition.

The removal restriction rule is already agreed; its strong consequences should appear in a worked example. The fear-source case still needs a decision.

### 18 End-of-combat cleanup needs a clear trigger

**Confirmed framework, incomplete entries.** Routine Burning/Freezing/Bleeding cleanup is assumed only when the needed help or method is available. A lone unconscious character cannot provide that help. Poison, wounds, Dying, and Last Words do not simply disappear because enemies are gone.

The eventual condition reference should include those assumptions explicitly. The simulated victories contain no ongoing damage, so their automatic stabilization/breath cleanup does not validate every real encounter ending.

### 19 Side-chosen turns alter duration and rescue opportunities

**Confirmed.** An ally can take their turn before a dying character to heal them before their Grit/death-roll choice. A defensive effect lasting until the start of the owner's next turn may cover different numbers of enemy actions depending on when that character acts in consecutive rounds.

This is useful tactical agency. It also means a fixed initiative simulation is inadequate. The model compares a simple urgent-healer selection policy with fixed order; actual table choices can be better or worse. Keep durations explicit rather than replacing them with a vague “one round.”

## Equipment and crafting checks

### 20 Supply size is a large multiplier, even though bulk stays one

**Confirmed.** Expected uses are d4: 4, d6: 10, d8: 18, d10: 28, d12: 40, d20: 60. A greater d8 potion has 4.5 times the expected healing supply of a lesser d4 bottle at the same bulk. A d4 bottle has a 68.36% chance to be empty after four uses; a d8 bottle only 1.80%.

Inventory dice are an uncertainty mechanic, not a one-dose consumable system. Price, rarity, carrying pressure, and recipe replenishment time need to reflect complete die progression.

### 21 Cumulative progress guarantees eventual completion when stakes do not interrupt

**Confirmed, excluding additional complications.** Every attempt contributes at least one. A target of 10 therefore takes at most ten attempts; at attribute 0 with neutral dice it averages 5.73 attempts. A derivation target of 365 averages about 199.37 attempts, not 365.

“Years to derive” requires a meaningful time per attempt as well as a high target. If an attempt takes one hour, a large target is still not automatically years. Daily wound treatment has a built-in calendar limit; crafting and derivation instead depend on their printed interval. Do not add a universal daily crafting restriction without agreement.

### 22 Once a recipe exists, derivation time no longer limits its spread

**Confirmed.** Recipes are reusable and shareable. One crafter can spend years deriving an item, then share the recipe with everyone qualified to use it. A found recipe bypasses derivation entirely.

This supports discovery and knowledge as treasure. It also means powerful-item balance must reside in actual crafting requirements, time, ingredients, and use gates, not solely in the difficulty of discovering the first recipe.

### 23 Replenishment can overwhelm depletion when foraging and crafting are easy

**Conditional risk.** A legal forage/craft route that restores a potion's inventory step between every fight can effectively refresh repeatable full healing. That is allowed if its recipes, environment, and time support it; it should not happen accidentally through unspecified times.

Tests should include both no resupply and a reliable resupply route. The campaign experiment includes a fully replenished-bottle control to show the upper bound, not to assert such a route is already available.

### 24 No attunement and broad wearable items require property budgeting

**Conditional risk.** Non-stacking matching bonuses prevents duplicate Resistance inflation, but different properties still accumulate: armor supplies physical protection, a belt fire protection, an eyeglass a task Boon, and a cloak a Reaction. Zero-bulk incidental items with powerful properties could evade inventory pressure if their cards do not assign appropriate bulk.

Retain the agreed no-attunement policy. Test complete equipment collections, their action competition, requirements, and acquisition. Do not assume “bonuses do not stack” limits every distinct benefit.

### 25 Weapon bulk belongs to action cards but must be counted consistently

**Unresolved.** The latest rule keeps ordinary weapon equipment descriptive and places bulk/type/requirements on the attack card. A single weapon may serve several attacks. If their bulk specifications differ, which bulk does the inventory record use before an attack is declared?

Also clarify the physical relationship between a flavor item, its compatible attack types, and any special additive property. Do not solve this by moving ordinary attack damage or range back onto weapon equipment; Bruce explicitly rejected that.

## Encounter and presentation checks

### 26 Fixed HP and modest damage growth put more weight on control at high level

**Conditional risk.** A Master effect that removes actions or grants several attacks can be much stronger than a small increase in damage, even with bounded HP. High-tier testing needs actual cards, not merely level labels and larger monsters. The current data cannot certify levels 3 and 7.

### 27 Fixed creature CV needs more than HP multiplied by damage

**Model finding.** Equal total enemy HP distributed among one, four, or twelve bodies produces very different exposure and wound rates. Summing a simple HP × per-action-damage product can miss that difference. Use durability, offense/action frequency, and control/support measurements separately when fitting a scalar CV.

Keep the agreed fixed printed CV and additive encounter sum as the first interface. If residual errors cluster around swarms, combinations, or party size, only then consider an encounter adjustment. There is not yet a defensible published CV formula.

### 28 Linear party scaling is a useful first approximation for duration, less so for wounds

**Model finding.** Scaling both ordinary creatures and PCs from two to eight maintains broadly similar round counts in the tested fixture, but the likelihood of somebody dropping changes substantially. Focus fire concentrates many attacks on a single HP pool; pooled party HP is not interchangeable with individual HP.

Preserve the provisional party-size formula while marking very small and large parties for testing. Do not use matching duration alone as proof of matching difficulty.

### 29 Forced-movement groups need a board walkthrough before a damage budget

**Unresolved.** Group collisions share the push allowance and each member takes damage. For three creatures colliding with a hard object with two squares of allowance left, the stated hard-object rule implies three damage each before Resistance: nine total from that collision alone. Earlier collisions may have already dealt damage.

The exact group footprint, square-entry payment versus collision timing, and final separation must be illustrated with a small grid. Otherwise the same push can produce different damage and illegal ending positions at different tables. The study does not invent a pinball geometry engine from incomplete rules.

### 30 The book needs one current specification before catalog migration

**Confirmed repository drift.** Current chapters still include skill bonuses, old HP formulas, Grit recovery, old condition effects, and incompatible equipment procedures. The conversation's later decisions supersede those texts. A designer could accidentally balance one subsystem against the older version of another.

Preserve this study as evidence. Before migrating chapters, consolidate the approved rules and unresolved choices into one current specification with traceable decisions. No canonical chapter has been changed by this study.

## What is working

- One roll and fixed outcome damage make exact analysis and fast reference possible.
- No automatic HP growth makes level-1 arithmetic useful later, provided control and multi-target effects are budgeted.
- Boons/Banes give strong, measurable changes without target-number arithmetic.
- Reciprocal grappling costs and non-chaining free attacks already prevent some obvious abuses.
- Inventory dice give supplies predictable long-run value with uncertainty at the table.
- Finite Grit and permanent wounds create a genuine distinction between recovering HP and escaping consequences.
- Explicit cards can hold exceptions without requiring new universal subsystems. The challenge is keeping their timing, prerequisites, and output budgets consistent.
