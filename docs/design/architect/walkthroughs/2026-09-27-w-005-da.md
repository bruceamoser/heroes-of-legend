**Instruments:** `scripts/da-walkthrough.py` (skill; the sweep, with 6 selftest controls) and

## What the walkthrough is, and what it is not

The bestiary already has three passes behind it: the conformance census (bands, DR ceilings, HP against
`20:659`), the pacing gate (`rounds-to-resolve.py`: 3.79 / 3.68 / 3.36 rounds, green), and the
attributes census. All three read the bestiary as a TABLE. This walkthrough reads it as a FIGHT: for
every block, at the band its Challenge is budgeted into, can the party the encounter builder will send
actually reduce its HP, and does the block's damage still discriminate once the party's DR is applied.

Coverage note, because the first run of my own sweep read **48**: the section-20 heading set is 49, and
any rating regex anchored on `(Challenge N)$` drops the Vrock (`=== Vrock (Demon, Challenge 6)`, a type
prefix). `rounds-to-resolve.py` was carrying exactly that regex, so **the pacing gate has been averaging
48 blocks while the book prints 49**; it also carried `CBAND Master = (7,8,9,10)`, which parsed the
Ancient Dragon (C12) and then left it out of every Master average. Both are fixed in the gate this
cycle (49/49, C7+ banded): the verdicts move Adept `3.68 -> 3.73` and Master `3.36 -> 3.27` and still
PASS, and the two numbers now describe the whole bestiary. My instrument additionally fails loudly if
its parse count ever differs from the heading count (exit 2, naming the unreadable heading), because a
silently smaller corpus reads as a clean bill.

47 of 49 blocks are sound: no block's printed traits remove the party's ability to reduce its HP, and
every block's offence keeps its three tiers distinct after the party's printed DR (light 1 / medium 2 /
heavy 3, `16:23`) is subtracted - measured across the full reachable range DR 0-3, 0 collapses. The two
failures are singletons, and each is a block whose own text does the damage.

## Failing block 1 - the Wraith (C3, Adept band)

The Wraith prints **two** reductions over the same population:

```
*HP 14, DR 3 (non-magical).*        <- 20:281
*Incorporeal:* half damage from non-magical physical attacks.   <- 20:285
```

In the book's own order - type modifier first, subtract DR, floor at 1 (`13:155`, ruled 2026-09-27) -
a mundane Adept hero's `5/8/11` resolves as `[1, 1, 2]`: 5 halves to 2, DR 3 takes it below the floor;
8 halves to 4, DR 3 leaves 1; 11 halves to 5, DR 3 leaves 2.

| roll | printed | half (round down) | - DR 3 | floored |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weak | 5 | 2 | -1 | 1 |
| Standard | 8 | 4 | 1 | 1 |
| Strong | 11 | 5 | 2 | 2 |

Played out over all 216 rolls of a competent +4 attack: **50.0% of every swing lands for exactly 1
damage and 50.0% for 2 - average 1.500/hit, HP 14, 9.3 hits to drop the thing.** The same hero holding
a magical weapon deals 9.444/hit (**6.30x**). Two outcomes separated by one point is not a damage
system; it is a coin flip with a rounding error.

The invariant broken is printed twice in the book. `16:25`: above the ceiling the three tiers of a band
"have to stay distinct"; `20:659`: a monster's DR cannot exceed its tier's ceiling (4 at Adept), and the
Wraith's DR 3 is inside it - it is the SECOND, redundant reduction that pushes the effective reduction
past the ceiling to roughly 7.5. The two lines are one statement printed twice, and the DR field is the
one the block's HP follows (`3 x (7.50 - 3) = 13.5 -> 14`). **Row 197.**

## Failing block 2 - the Swarm of Rats (C1/2, Novice band)

```
*Bites:* Automatic 1 damage to all in space.
*Immune:* Single-target attacks.                                <- 20:78
*Vulnerable: Area Effects (takes double damage from area effects).*   <- 20:80
```

The block's own counter is an area effect, and **no area effect is reachable at the band the block is
budgeted into.** Novice is levels 1-2, where a card costs one Discipline rank. Book-wide there are
exactly two cards carrying the `Area` keyword: `09:130` Spell Storm (a Novice talent, once per session,
and it spreads a single-target spell's result to *other* targets - the primary hit is still a
single-target attack, so it does not land on the swarm unless the swarm was a secondary) and `09:382`
Cyclone (3 Melee, Master). No rank-1 class ability targets more than one creature.

So a Novice party deals this block **zero damage, ever**. Played against the Standard budget at level 1
(`1.5 x 1 x 4 = 6`, i.e. **twelve** of a C1/2 creature):

- 204 HP the party cannot touch, 12 automatic damage a round that DR does not apply to, and a party
  pool of `12 x (2 + 1) = 36` per hero, **144 for four: the party is ground to nothing in 12 rounds
  having dealt no damage at all.** The fight has no resolution path other than flight, and 1 automatic
  damage per swarm per round means fleeing is the only move the party can make.

The book prints the opposite expectation for exactly this creature class at `19:59`: "A swarm of minions
needs low HP so they drop fast and the action economy swings back toward the party." The honest repair
is therefore not to delete the swarm's identity but to print its reduction once, in the book's own
resistance idiom (`20:285` Incorporeal, `17:382` a hero item's "resistance to non-magical bludgeoning,
piercing, and slashing damage"): `*Swarming:* half damage from slashing, piercing, and bludgeoning
attacks.` - halved weapon damage, full damage from fire and magic, double from an area effect. **Row 198.**

## The other direction - is the bestiary winnable, and does anything trivialise it

Played, not computed: the book's OWN printed Standard example at level 3 (`20:661`: 2 Knights + 2
Basilisks + 2 Green Hags, all C3, budget `1.5 x 3 x 4 = 18`). Four Adept heroes (HP 14, DR 2, attack
`3d6 + 4`, defence `3d6 + Dodge 2 + Agility 1 - Challenge 2`), attacks read by their tiers, defence
reverse-mapped to the monster's Weak/Standard/Strong, DR subtracted with the floor of 1, Grit spent at
0 HP as printed (`13:338`).

```
monster HP total 84 -> 0        rounds played: 4      (pacing window 3-4: inside it)
party pool 4 x 56 = 224         damage taken: 54 = 24% of the party's total pool
the hero the monsters focused spent 40 of their 56 = 71%; the other three spent 14 between them
```

Rounds 1-4, monsters dropped in order: two Knights in round 1 (22 damage focused), Basilisk A round 2,
Basilisk B round 3, both Hags rounds 3-4. Nothing trivialised it: every block dealt its row, no hero was
removed (nobody ran out of Grit), and the fight ends inside the printed window. The one thing the play
shows is that the printed "about three quarters of the day's staying power" is a claim about the hero
the monsters FOCUS, not about the party's total pool: 71% against 24%. That is a reading of `19:53`, not
a defect in it, and it needed no repair.

## Verdict

**BAD on 2 of 49 blocks, GOOD on the other 47.** Both failures are the same shape: a block prints two
statements of one reduction, or an immunity whose named counter does not exist where the block is used.
Neither needs a new mechanic. Both repairs are the book's own printed idioms applied to the block that
invented a second copy of them. Filed as #715, one branch, one file.

## What this walkthrough does NOT cover, stated so it is not read as a clean bill

- It models mundane physical offence and the band rows only. It does not price a party's conditions,
  control or action denial, and it does not model multiattack per round (the pacing gate does).
- "Reachable at the band" was checked against cards, and against class abilities at rank 1. It does not
  attempt to enumerate item-and-terrain combinations a clever party could assemble; the standard is what
  the book prints as reachable, which is what a new player has.
- The sweep's trait vocabulary is the set a stat block uses to reduce damage (DR and its parenthetical
  scope, Incorporeal, Vulnerable, Immune, Regeneration, Undead Nature). An effect that removes the
  party's ability to act by other means (a permanent `Invisible` Pixie, the Basilisk's two-stage gaze)
  is a Contrarian question, not this instrument's, and is left to the lens.
