# Bestiary family survey — October 15, 2026

Bruce asked for recognizable classic creatures and deeper families, using the
supplied Pathfinder Second Edition Bestiary 1 as a coverage guide. This pass adds
108 original HOL entries to the existing 84. This is a curated core roster, not a
claim that every Pathfinder creature or age variant has been reproduced.

## What was counted

The supplied **Pathfinder Bestiary (2019), PZO2102E.pdf** has 362 PDF pages. Its
“Creatures by Level” index, printed pp. 350–357 (PDF pp. 351–358), lists **411 stat
blocks**, grouped under **185 distinct category labels** after ignoring letter case.
Those labels are not all biological families: “Dragon, chromatic” is one label,
while a standalone named creature can also be a label. Do not compare 185 labels
to a count of HOL creature types.

The index identifies **18 type groups**. These are counted from the index’s Type
Trait column, not inferred from a creature’s name. The “Creatures by Type” spread,
printed pp. 348–349 (PDF pp. 349–350), was also inspected as a visual reference.

| Pathfinder index type | Stat blocks | HOL treatment |

## Family lessons actually consulted

In addition to both index spreads, the detailed creature passages consulted were
Ghost (printed pp. 166–167), Ghoul/Ghast (168–169), Goblin (180–181), the opening
Giant material (170), Ogre (252–253) and Skeleton (298–299). These give useful
contrasts: ordinary and elite guards, ranged support, spellcasters, leaders,
mobile hunters, foul-breath predators, corporeal versus incorporeal threats, and
size variants with different reach. Other names in this expansion were selected
from the index for coverage; their Pathfinder stat blocks were not converted.

HOL uses original descriptions and encounter situations. No Pathfinder AC, levels,
spell slots, damage dice, action counts, alignment rules or disease tracks are
imported. Life, mental, physical and existing elemental damage types remain in
use. Conditions and the player-defense roll do the mechanical work. A ghast’s
stench uses Poisoned; a cockatrice’s temporary numbing attack uses Paralyzed rather
than silently establishing permanent petrification. Corrosion slows or obstructs;
it does not create an unapproved equipment-destruction rule.

## Deliberate limits and remaining work

- This is 192 distinct cards, not 411 Pathfinder conversions. Full dragon age
  ladders, extensive dinosaurs, lycanthrope infection and advancement, deeper aquatic societies and
  setting-specific outsiders remain possible later expansions.
- The 108 additions, their explicit summon permissions and their CV inputs are
  proposals. Source-game levels do not determine HOL CV or summon tier.
- Existing open questions on summoned healing and collision timing remain open.
  This pass adds no new general exception to settle them.
- The supplied Bestiary 2 and Bestiary 3 are available for later gap passes. They
  were not used as evidence for this Bestiary 1 count or silently claimed read.
