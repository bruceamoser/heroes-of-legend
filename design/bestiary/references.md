# Bestiary research notes

Consulted October 13, 2026 through Bruce’s local RPG library. These are design
references, not sources for copied stat blocks. Creature descriptions, numbers and
abilities in this draft are original HOL expressions. No foreign action economy,
AC, spell slots, challenge rating or damage dice are imported.

Page locators below are **PDF page numbers**, so they work despite differing printed
pagination. Local paths are recorded as code to avoid making the authoring link audit
depend on a mounted external library.

| Reference | Pages consulted for this pass | Useful design lesson | HOL expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| D&D 5e, Monster Manual | PDF 7–9, 12, 292; Statistics, actions/limited use, Troll | Separate movement, senses, traits and actions; a recognizable monster has a recognizable counter | Full card fields; explicit opportunity profile; troll regeneration suppressed by fire, but still death at 0 |
| Dungeon World | PDF 226, 235–236; monster creation and cavern dwellers | Begin with desire and distinctive fictional behavior rather than an arithmetic chassis | Every entry has purpose, signs, tactics and a way to change the encounter |
| Pathfinder RPG Bestiary, third printing (first edition) | PDF 299–300; universal monster rules | Reusable monster capabilities need precise scope and clear entry-specific values | Separate monster-ability library, with immediate rules repeated on the creature |
| Savage Worlds Adventure Edition | PDF 177–178; Designing Threats and Special Abilities | Adversaries need capabilities that serve the scene, not player-character construction | No DP builds for monsters; lean action sets, explicit special movement and vulnerabilities |

## Deliberate departures

HOL keeps attacks productive, makes armor resistance, and leaves the dice with
players. It has no monster attack bonus against AC, automatic failed-save death,
ability-score drain, blanket immunity to ordinary weapons, free legendary turns,
or autonomous summoned army. A petrifying creature warns heroes and applies a
bounded condition before a dangerous follow-up. A dragon moves the battlefield
and threatens an area rather than taking an unexplained handful of extra turns.

The roster covers familiar fantasy functions: hungry animals, disciplined troops,
ambush predators, restless dead, bargains with fey, elemental hazards, made guardians,
ruin dwellers, huge brutes and exceptional villains. Traditional names are used
where useful; setting-specific proprietary creatures are not transplanted.
