# Equipment and Recipes

A coil of rope is easy to overlook until someone is hanging above a ravine. Choose
equipment for the journey you expect, leave room for what you might find, and know
which hand will reach your potion when the fight goes badly.

## Coins and trade

HOL uses one currency: **coins**. A listed price buys the described ordinary item or
full supply. Choose armor that matches the training you’ve learned.

Useful intact gear sells for **half its listed value**, rounded down, or trades at
**full value** with a willing trader. A 12-coin weapon sells for 6 or trades for
12 coins of goods. Pay any shortfall in coins; excess trade value is not cash change.
A merchant does not have unlimited stock or an obligation to buy everything you make.

## Armor and training

Choose a suit of armor to match your training and acquire it with your other equipment. Its resistance applies only while worn and with the matching learned training.

| Training | Requirement | DP | Physical resistance |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| [Armored Training I](../../disciplines/armor.md) | Study Armor | 1 | Light: 1 |
| Armored Training II | Armored Training I | 2 | Medium: 2 |
| Armored Training III | Armored Training II | 2 | Heavy: 3 |

All three may be learned at level 0. Each new step lets you use its armor category as well as any you learned earlier. Use the highest applicable resistance, never their sum. Attacks deal at least 1 damage after resistance if any damaging component survives immunity.

## Bulk and hands

Record each carried item's **Bulk**. Most individual equipment entries and supply
bundles use 1 Bulk; the entry tells you otherwise. A pack adds its own Bulk and does
not erase its contents. Capacity is 10 + 3 × Brawn. Each Bulk above capacity reduces
Speed by 1, to a minimum of 0.

A single compatible weapon serves all the attacks that can use it. Ordinary weapon
appearance changes no mechanics. A flaming melee weapon can add its printed fire
effect to a compatible attack; it cannot become a ranged weapon simply because
you chose a ranged card.

Track occupied hands. Healing requires a free hand. A bow, two-handed weapon, or
weapon-and-shield combination leaves none. Drawing for an attack is part of that
attack, dropping is free, and proper stowing or picking up costs a Maneuver. There
is no automatic free grip-change exception.

## Inventory dice

A supply bundle has an inventory die instead of a counted number of uses. After
using it, roll that die. On a **1**, reduce it one step. On any other result it stays
where it is. The use you just completed still works.

**d20 → d12 → d10 → d8 → d6 → d4 → out**

Check at the interval printed on the supply: ammunition when used, food by its
person-day rule, lighting by its hour, and so on. A d4 healing bottle has a one-in-four
chance of becoming empty after each use. It is not a promise of four doses.

> **Example — the last swallow.** You drink a lesser healing bottle and restore its
> 6 HP, reduced by Life resistance if applicable. Then roll its d4 inventory die.
> A 1 empties the bottle; you still received that healing. At full HP the bottle
> cannot heal beyond your maximum.

Recipes raise an existing supply by their permitted number of steps, up to the
recipe's cap. A store refill uses the listed policy. Do not turn unknown leftovers
into additional full bottles by splitting them among containers.

## Special equipment and understanding

A special item adds only the effects it prints: damage, resistance, an ability or
another explicit benefit. Apply normal nonstacking rules to matching numerical
bonuses. Different damage-type resistances can coexist; use the highest of each.

The item states whether understanding is required. Identification abilities reveal
different kinds of information. Knowing a recipe is a separate permission from
knowing what an item does. The recipe section lists tools, materials, time and what
the crafting result produces; none of those are supplied by the recipe's name alone.

### Items that change attributes

**Add a modifier.** For each attribute, add the highest applicable item bonus
and subtract the largest applicable item penalty. Published item modifiers range
from −2 to +2. Your effective attribute can go beyond the creation range.

**Cursed penalties.** If a cursed item penalizes an attribute, apply the largest
applicable item penalty and ignore item bonuses to that attribute while the curse
applies.

**Set a value.** If an item sets an attribute to a specific value, use that exact
value while the item applies. It replaces your base value and every bonus and
penalty to that attribute. You can use one item that sets a given attribute at a
time. Apply any exception printed on a curse card, such as suppressing item bonuses
or taking precedence over other effects.

> With Brawn +1 and item bonuses of +2 and +1, your effective Brawn is +3. A cursed
> item with a −1 Brawn penalty suppresses both bonuses and brings your Brawn to 0.
> An item that sets Brawn to +3 gives you exactly +3 unless the curse overrides it.
