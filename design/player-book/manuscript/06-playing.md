# Playing the Game

A drawn blade changes the conversation. A broken bridge changes the journey. The
rules help you decide what those changes mean when timing, danger and the choices
of several characters meet.

Begin with what your hero is doing. You can use a learned ability, take a universal
action, or describe something the situation makes possible. The DA establishes the
circumstances and calls for a roll only when uncertainty has consequences.

## Resolve an action

When you use an ability, declare it **before rolling**. Read its requirements, choose
its targets and any printed options, and check range and equipment. Spend the action
and any required cost. Roll its attribute if instructed, then apply the complete
Weak, Standard or Strong outcome. Finally, record conditions and continuing effects.

An outcome is complete: do not add the Weak damage to the Standard damage. If a
Strong outcome includes an earlier benefit, it says so. Instant damage happens once;
a condition applied by that damage follows the condition's own timing.

A no-roll ability still costs its printed action. A treatment that takes an hour
cannot be completed in one combat turn merely because it is indexed as a Main Action.

## Boons, Banes and results

Roll three dice, plus one die for each net Boon or Bane, to a maximum of six dice.
With Boons, retain the best three; with Banes, retain the worst three. Add the
printed attribute once. Weak is 8 or less, Standard 9–14, and Strong 15 or more.

| Circumstance | Usual adjustment |
|---|---|
| Meaningful help or obstacle | 1 Boon or Bane |
| Substantial help or a severe obstacle | 2 Boons or Banes |
| Exceptional advantage or difficulty, still possible | 3 Boons or Banes |

These are guides for the DA. They do not turn an impossible attempt into a possible
one. Do not count the same obstacle twice under different names. Boons and Banes
from distinct valid sources stack, cancel, and then use the three-die net limit.

> **Example — keeping the best three.** An attack has 3 Boons and 1 Bane, leaving
> 2 Boons. Roll five dice. On 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, retain 4, 5, 6 for 15. Brawn +1 makes
> 16, a Strong result. If two Banes remained instead, retain 1, 2, 4 for 7; Brawn +1
> would give 8, a Weak result. Neither roll changes the attack's printed damage.

Read retained natural 111 as Weak with an additional complication and 666 as Strong
with an additional benefit. The result still makes its printed progress. The DA
fits a benefit or complication to the scene; it does not silently double damage or
grant another action.

## Taking turns

At the start of combat, one player rolls unmodified 3d6. On 12 or more, the player
side acts first. Otherwise the opposing side begins. This determines which side
starts **every round of this combat**; do not roll again each round.

The starting side chooses one of its characters to act. Then the other side chooses
one. Alternate until everyone has taken a turn. If one side has no characters left
to act, the other finishes its remaining turns. A character takes one turn each round.
Each side chooses its order as it goes.

On your turn, you have one **Main Action**, one **Maneuver**, and movement up to your
**Speed**. You also have one **Reaction**, available for its stated trigger. Refresh
that turn's budget at the start of your turn. A defense roll does not itself spend
a Reaction. Choose your retained categories at turn start if Dazed or Grappled.

You can split movement around your actions. You can convert your Main Action into
a Maneuver and a Maneuver into another movement allowance. Conversion goes only
in that direction. Converting your Main and original Maneuver into movement gives
three total movement allowances: a full sprint.

> **Example — reaching an ally.** Speed 6 lets Mara move 6 squares. She converts her
> Maneuver into movement for 6 more, then uses her Main Action to heal a companion.
> She has not gained a second Main Action, and any difficult terrain costs still apply.

A Reaction resolves when its trigger says it does, including on someone else's turn.
Once spent, it is unavailable until refreshed. A free attack granted by an ability
spends no action and may occur more than once in a turn, but **free attacks never
trigger additional free attacks**. Read the granting effect to see which attack it allows.

## Attacking and defending

Players roll. When your hero attacks, roll the attack's printed attribute and read
its attacker outcomes. This is true even if the target is another player character;
the receiving player does not also roll a defense.

When a DA-controlled creature attacks you, roll the defense attribute specified by
that attack. It might be Agility to evade a blow, Fortitude to withstand a bodily
assault, or another attribute named by the attack. Apply the result from its
**Weak defense / Standard defense / Strong defense** columns. Weak defense is the
most dangerous outcome; Strong defense is the least dangerous.

| Player's defense | Equivalent attacker outcome |
|---|---|
| Weak | Strong attack |
| Standard | Standard attack |
| Strong | Weak attack |

Creature **Evasion** adds its printed Boons or Banes to your attack. **Threat** adds
its printed Boons or Banes to your defense against that creature's attack. These
use the ordinary dice rules; do not add them numerically to the total.

If you control a summon, roll that creature's printed attributes. Its player profile
already presents attack outcomes in the correct direction. Your hero's own Brawn
or Reason does not replace the summon’s value.

## Damage and resistance

Damage comes from the chosen outcome, not a weapon damage die. Your attribute has
already affected the roll; do not add it again to damage. Apply each matching
resistance after determining the outcome. **Armor DR is physical resistance.**

Use the highest applicable resistance of each type, to a maximum of 3. Two sources
of physical resistance 2 do not give 4. Physical resistance 2 and Fire resistance 1
can apply together because they protect against different types.

A damaging event always deals at least **1 total damage** after resistance. This
minimum applies to the event, not separately to every component. It does not create
damage for a harmless ability. Conditions, pushes and other riders still apply
unless their own rules prevent them.

> **Example — steel and flame.** An attack deals 3 physical and 1 fire damage.
> Physical resistance 2 reduces the physical portion to 1. Fire resistance 1 removes
> the fire portion. Take 1 total damage, not one of each type. If both portions had
> been reduced to zero, the event would still deal its minimum 1 total damage.

Physical includes impacts, sonic force and supernatural force when the attack says
physical. Fire, Cold and Lightning have separate resistances even though their wounds
share the Elemental table. Life resistance also reduces ordinary HP healing.

Same-kind numerical bonuses do not add together unless explicitly stated. Conditions
with meaningful ratings can add; binary Prone cannot. Boons and Banes use their own
stacking rules. Where a creature has a weakness, use its printed procedure; do not
invent extra damage simply from a descriptive vulnerability.

## Shields and guarded positions

Defend is a universal Maneuver that gives 1 Boon to defenses until the start of your
next turn. Repeating Defend does not increase that same benefit.

Shield Block spends a Reaction before an eligible defense. While you use the held
shield, gain 1 Boon to defenses against attacks the DA judges it can block, until
the start of your next turn. Most ordinary physical attacks can be blocked. Defend
and Shield Block are separate sources: together they supply 2 Boons. Creatures use
shields the same way.

## Opportunity attacks

Leaving an enemy's melee reach voluntarily, casting beside it, standing, stowing an
item, or retrieving an item can provoke. The enemy may spend its Reaction to make
**Basic Melee**, never a specialty card. Resolve that attack **at the start of the
provoking action**, before the original action takes effect.

Then continue the original action if its actor is capable. Damage alone does not
cancel it. An actor reduced to Dying cannot continue. Commit the action on declaration;
if interrupted permanently, that action is spent. Additional HP or item costs are
paid only when the action continues into resolution. An unspent potion is not drunk
by an interrupted character who can no longer act.

Drawing a weapon as part of attacking adds no separate action. Dropping an item is
free. Stowing or picking up an item costs a Maneuver. Forced movement never provokes.
An ability can explicitly allow movement without provoking.

> **Example — a spell under pressure.** Vaelis begins casting beside an armed foe.
> The foe spends its Reaction on Basic Melee. Vaelis makes the required defense and
> loses HP. Still conscious and otherwise capable, Vaelis continues the spell and
> pays any remaining printed cost. A healing spell cannot proceed if its upfront
> life cost would leave its caster at 0 HP.

## Distance, space and cover

All distances use **squares**. A square is the useful unit for your map; it does not
require a fixed conversion into feet or meters. A diagonal step costs one square.
Normal ground costs 1 movement; difficult ground costs 2.

You cannot move through an occupied square unless its occupant is Prone. That square
costs 2 movement, and you cannot end there. Standing costs 2 movement. An ability
that moves you without provoking still respects terrain, occupied spaces and legal
endpoints unless it explicitly says otherwise.

A target you must see must actually be visible. An attack also needs a path to it.
Partial obstruction gives **1 Bane**; heavy cover gives **3 Banes**. Heavy cover still
leaves a narrow attack path, such as fighting from behind a wall or dense thicket.
A solid barrier with no attack path prevents that attack. Concealment that prevents
you from seeing a sight-required target is not merely heavy cover.

For a player area attack, choose the printed range, shape, inclusion and exclusions.
Roll once using the **worst applicable net Boon/Bane situation** among its targets.
Use that result for all targets; apply their resistances individually. When an NPC
area attack affects several players, each player makes their own defense roll.

## Push, pull and throw

A push moves a target away; a pull brings it toward the source. Follow a card's
specific direction and shape. Apply **Stability** once to the initial target, reducing
the available forced movement, to a minimum of 0. Stability is normally 0; only an
explicit grant increases it. Use the highest source, maximum 3.

Spend the entered square's terrain cost from the movement budget. If any positive
budget remains, the target enters the final square even if its cost exceeds that
budget, then stops. For example, push 1 can enter one difficult square and stop.
Stability does not reduce Prone, conditions, voluntary movement, projectile range,
obstacle progress or the reduction of a grapple.

A collision with another movable creature forms a moving group. Apply collision
damage to the participants using movement remaining on contact, then subtract the
new creature's Stability once before continuing. The group moves together; do not
charge the same creature's Stability twice in that movement. A hard obstruction
stops the group and deals remaining-budget collision damage +1 to each member.
The original mover then bounces one square in the opposite direction if possible.

> **Example — Stability in a chain.** Push 5 strikes Stability 1, leaving 4. After
> one normal square, 3 remains. The moving creature strikes a new creature with
> Stability 2. Resolve the contact, subtract 2, and use the remaining 1 to move the
> group one further normal square. If the attack also inflicted Burning, stopping
> the movement would not remove that condition.

> **Playtest ruling — crowded collisions.** Never finish with overlapping occupants.
> If a large moving group cannot fit, the DA stops it before illegal overlap and
> places its members in the nearest legal squares along the route. This is a
> temporary geometry ruling, not permission to pass through a wall or standing creature.

## Grappling and capture

Grappling requires a learned ability; it is not automatically granted with Basic Melee.
Grappled prevents movement. At ratings 1, 2 and 3, retain respectively two, one or
none of Main Action, Maneuver and Reaction, chosen at turn start. The grappler is
restricted by their own contribution as well. Multiple grapplers' contributions add
on the target. Attacks against a Grappled target gain Boons equal to its rating.

If you still have a Main Action, Force can reduce your grapple by 1/2/3. Grappled 3
needs outside help. A grappler can freely release their own contribution. A grapple
is maintained and does not simply decay at turn end.

Attacks are lethal by default. Taking a prisoner requires a printed nonlethal ability,
grappling or restraint. Once a helpless captive is secured and no combat remains,
return to ordinary narration. You do not need to keep taking turns to conduct a conversation.

## Ending a fight

A fight ends when the immediate opposition is resolved, not because everyone decides
to ignore an active death roll. Resolve Dying before leaving combat timing. When it
is safe to pause and companions can attend to an ordinary fire or similar immediate
hazard, settle that hazard as the scene allows. Other conditions remain until their
own removal, expiry or remedy. A safe breath restores HP; it does not remove wounds,
refund Grit, cure every condition or reverse Last Words.
