# Welcome to Heroes of Legend

The door has held for a hundred years. Tonight, someone on the other side is knocking.
Your companions look at you. You have a lantern, a scar that aches before rain, and
one very bad idea that might get everyone through this alive.

**Heroes of Legend** is a fantasy roleplaying game about people who become remarkable
through the choices they make. You might be a scholar who reads an opponent's stance
like a page, a guardian who plants a shield in the only safe doorway, or a wanderer
who calls the forest to your friends' defense. Your character is built from ancestry,
experience, class and learned abilities. The combinations belong to you.

## At the table

Each player speaks and acts for one hero. One participant is the **Dungeon Architect**,
or **DA**: the person who describes the world, portrays the people and creatures in
it, presents danger, and adjudicates uncertain situations. The DA tells you what your
hero can perceive. You say what you try. Together, you discover what happens.

You do not need to act in a voice, memorize this book, or find a single correct
solution. Ask questions. Describe a practical approach. Listen to what your companions
want to attempt. A good plan can change the situation before anyone touches a die.

You will need three six-sided dice, and ideally three more for Boons and Banes.
Consumable supplies use d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 and d20 inventory dice. Bring a character
sheet, your learned abilities, something to record changes, and tokens or miniatures
when a scene benefits from a square grid. A virtual tabletop can do the same work.

## The promise of the game

Character development rewards planning. You choose which disciplines to study and
which abilities make those studies useful. At the table, the chosen ability tells
you what to do: its action, attribute, range, outcomes and limits are printed together.
Your sword's appearance is yours to invent; the attack describes what it accomplishes.

Attacks connect. A poor roll changes how much harm and pressure an attack creates;
it does not usually erase the turn. A stronger result may push an enemy away, expose
a weakness, leave them burning, or put an ally where they need to be.

You recover HP after a safe breath, but wounds and spent Grit follow you. Victories
can leave permanent scars. A hero who rises again is not untouched by what happened.

## Learn one roll

When an uncertain action has consequences, roll **3d6 + one attribute**. The six
attributes are Brawn, Fortitude, Agility, Guile, Knowledge and Reason. An ability names
its attribute; otherwise the DA calls for the attribute that fits the attempt.

| Total | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 8 or less | Weak | Make the printed progress; you have not necessarily completed your purpose. |
| 9–14 | Standard | Apply the Standard outcome. |
| 15 or more | Strong | Apply the Strong outcome. |

Three retained natural ones give Weak plus a complication. Three retained natural
sixes give Strong plus a benefit. These exceptional results use the numbers on the
dice, before adding your attribute.

**Boons** help and **Banes** hinder. Cancel one against one. Each remaining Boon adds
a die; keep the highest three. Each remaining Bane adds a die; keep the lowest three.
Use at most three net extra dice. Add your attribute after choosing the three dice.
Difficulty changes the dice you keep, not the Weak/Standard/Strong boundaries.

> **Example — a dangerous crossing.** Lira has Agility +2. The DA asks for Agility
> to cross a crumbling beam before the pursuers arrive. The rotten surface adds
> 1 Bane; an ally's secured rope supplies 1 Boon. They cancel. Lira rolls 2, 4, 5:
> 11 + 2 = 13, a Standard result. She reaches the far ledge as agreed before the roll.
> If there were no danger or meaningful cost, the group could simply describe crossing.

## A moment of play

**DA:** The broken bridge ends two squares short of the far bank. Water roars below.
The rider behind you is reaching for a horn.

**Lira:** I have room to run. Can I jump the gap?

**DA:** Your Agility is +2, so your running broad jump reaches four squares. There is
a clear landing. Spend the movement and you are across; no roll needed.

**Borin:** I cannot leave Oren behind. I plant myself between him and the rider.
I use Defend and keep my Reaction ready.

**Oren:** I use Force on the jammed bridge mechanism. Can we lower the remaining span?

**DA:** Yes. It needs two progress. A Weak result loosens it, but the rider will have
time to sound the horn. A Standard or Strong result opens it now.

**Oren:** Brawn +0. I roll 3, 4, 5: twelve. Standard, two progress.

**DA:** The catch tears loose. The span drops with a crack, giving you a route across.
The rider lowers the horn and draws a blade instead.

This is play: make choices in the fiction, apply the rule that fits, and let the
result change what can happen next. Force is an action Oren chooses. Brawn is the
attribute the action tells him to roll.

## Find your way through the book

Read Create Your Hero to build from the beginning, or choose one of the six ready-to-play
heroes near the back. Ancestries, origins and classes supply your first choices.
Disciplines show how to qualify for learned abilities. Playing the Game explains
combat; Beyond the Battlefield covers the rest of an adventurer's life.

Keep your own abilities close at hand. You do not need to memorize everyone else's
catalog. The universal actions are available to every character, provided they have
any required equipment. A learned card does not replace the ordinary permission to
try something anyone could reasonably attempt.

## Before the first adventure

Agree on the tone of the campaign and why your heroes travel together. Discuss what
kinds of danger, injury and horror people want included or left offstage. Anyone can
ask to pause a scene or change an uncomfortable detail. The wound tables describe
fictional injuries; their descriptions can be adjusted while retaining the rules.

Choose names, something each hero wants, and a connection between companions. Decide
how to share maps and record supplies. The DA should explain what your characters
already know about the first destination. You can discover the rest in play.
