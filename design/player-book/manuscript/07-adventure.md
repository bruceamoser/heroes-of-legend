# Beyond the Battlefield

The road matters. So do the people at its end. A warm welcome, a sound bridge and
a name remembered at the right moment can carry your company farther than another
weapon. Your abilities include ways to navigate these scenes, but you always begin
with what your hero says and does.

## Explore with purpose

Tell the DA what you examine, how you approach, and what you hope to learn. If lifting
the rug reveals the trapdoor, you find it by lifting the rug. No perception roll is
needed to discover a fact your action plainly reveals.

When searching involves uncertainty and a meaningful consequence, the DA calls for
**Reason**. When someone might perceive your concealed hero, roll **Agility** to
remain Hidden; the observer does not make an opposed roll. You need a plausible
place to hide. Dice do not make you disappear in open view.

Active searching or attempting to hide in combat costs a Main Action unless an
ability says otherwise. Hidden is universal, not separately tracked for each observer.
Attacking or openly revealing yourself ends it. The DA judges whether movement
maintains actual concealment. Invisible is different: you cannot be seen, but your
location is not necessarily unknown.

Before an unscripted roll, establish what progress means and what the consequences
are. A Weak search might narrow the location without exposing the answer; a Weak
concealment attempt might reach cover while leaving evidence of where you went.
These examples are possibilities, not automatic effects that override the scene.

## Consequences that matter

A **consequence** is what is at stake: injury, lost time, exposure, spent supplies,
a worse position or a closing opportunity. A **complication** adds a problem while
preserving any progress already earned. A **benefit** gives a useful advantage.
The DA explains the stakes before asking for the roll.

If you can repeat an attempt indefinitely without cost, danger or changed circumstances,
the table may be better served by describing the time and completing it. If failure
would seal an escape route or alert a guard, say so before rolling. Weak always means
its stated progress, not an unannounced success or an automatic failure.

## Work as a group

Group checks occur **outside combat**. The DA sets a progress target and states
whether the task is **binary** or **cumulative**. Each participating character rolls
an appropriate attribute, contributing 1 progress on Weak, 2 on Standard, or 3 on Strong.
Add the contributions and compare the total to the target.

For a binary task, meet the target in that attempt or the task is not completed.
For a cumulative task, retain progress between attempts as the task allows. The DA
states the time and consequence of each attempt. You can provide Aid or contribute
your own group roll, not both to the same attempt.

> **Example — the swollen gate.** Three heroes heave against a gate before a patrol
> arrives. The DA sets a binary target of 7. Weak, Strong and Strong contribute
> 1 + 3 + 3 = 7: the gate opens. Weak, Standard and Strong would total 6; the gate
> would stay shut and the declared consequence would follow. Pushing a boulder
> uphill might instead retain those 6 progress for a later attempt.

Crafting, deriving recipes and treatment do not use group checks. Their
individual procedures and daily limits remain in force.

## Travel and the outdoors

Ordinary travel does not require routine rolls, and HOL has no exhaustion track.
The DA calls for a roll when weather, pursuit, a dangerous route or another specific
circumstance creates a consequence. Supplies and time still matter. Decide what your
company carries, shares, buys or obtains with its abilities.

Foraging expertise comes from an appropriate environment card. It is not a universal
permission to identify every edible mushroom. Other heroes can still inspect the
land, collect something they already recognize, or help in a way the scene permits.
Specialist cards provide their own outcomes and limitations.

Climbing and swimming normally cost **2 movement per square**, like difficult terrain.
This does not make a sheer unsupported wall climbable or an impassable current safe.
A specialist ability can improve the activity. Roll only when the execution is
uncertain and the consequence matters.

## Jumping and falling

A running broad jump reaches **max(1, 2 + Agility) squares**. It needs two squares of
approach; without that approach, halve the maximum, rounding down to a minimum of 1.
A high jump reaches **max(0, 1 + floor(Agility ÷ 2)) squares** and needs no approach.
Spend movement for the distance traveled and land in a legal, supported, free square.

| Agility | Running broad jump | Standing broad jump | High jump |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| −2 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| −1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 0 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| +1 | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| +2 | 4 | 2 | 2 |

Zero high-jump squares does not mean you cannot hop over a low obstacle. The grid
measures large clearances. Safe jumps within your limits need no roll; a risky
landing can call for Agility. Jumping does not grant extra movement or passage
through a barrier.

Falling deals **3 physical damage per complete 2 squares fallen**. Round the distance
down to complete pairs before multiplying; normal physical resistance applies.
A fall of 5 squares deals 6 damage before resistance. A fall under 2 squares has
no automatic falling damage. Exceptional surfaces and landing circumstances are DA rulings.

## Talk, bargain and persuade

Describe what you want and why the listener might agree. A letter, an offer or
a demonstration can settle the matter outright. When it takes several exchanges,
agree on the concession and who can grant it. The DA sets the progress target
and tells you which approaches could work.

One meaningful exchange usually takes about a minute, or a Main Action in
combat. Use the attribute and outcomes of a learned Influence ability, or roll
the attribute the DA assigns for ordinary progress of 1/2/3. Bring a new fact,
offer or change in the situation for each further roll. Speakers working toward
the same concession can share progress. Listeners with different interests
may need separate objectives.

At the target, the listener grants the agreed concession. Each player still
decides what their own hero thinks and does. See [Influence](../../disciplines/influence.md)
for techniques that improve your arguments, distractions and threats.

## Magic in your hands

Learn spells from the pages of your spellbooks. To cast one, spend its printed
SP cost and follow its action and outcome. An attack spell deals its Weak damage
on a Weak roll.

You choose a card before rolling. A line, rectangle or placed area must use its
printed dimensions and range. Connected placed squares share edges. Decide targets
and any damage-type choice before rolling. An illustration or evocative description
does not silently make the area larger or add a condition.

To unravel a spell, use a spell or ability that supplies cancellation progress.
The usual target is 2 + the affected spell's printed SP cost. Casting beside
an enemy can provoke an opportunity attack. Resolve it before paying SP and
continuing the spell.

Beast forms keep your own HP, attributes and scars. They are not replaceable HP
pools. Use the form's printed actions and equipment restrictions. A form does not
import an entire monster entry or grant spellcasting if its rules forbid it.

For casting actions, Rituals, sustained magic and spellbook learning, use [Magic and spellbooks](../../rules/magic.md).

## Healing spends spell points

Each spell costs its printed SP. Page 1 of every spellbook costs **0 SP**. Your reserve starts at 6 SP at level 1 before explicit improvements.
Pay the full SP cost before rolling, after resolving any opportunity attack.
Healing requires a free hand. Restore the printed HP, apply the
recipient's Life resistance, and restore at least 1 if HP is missing. Never exceed
maximum HP. A Dying or stabilized living recipient needs no consent or Reaction.
Ordinary healing erases no scars and cannot restore the dead; only an explicit resurrection working can do so. Potions use their own fixed
healing and inventory die, without a spell outcome roll or SP cost.

> **Example: a finite reserve.** A Shepherd with 6 SP uses Mend for 1 SP. A Standard result restores 6 HP; Life resistance 2 reduces that to 4. The healer has 5 SP left. Restoring HP cannot replenish SP.

A healing spell can attack a creature whose profile says healing harms it. Use the
spell’s attribute, range and SP cost. Its HP result becomes Life damage; beneficial
recovery riders do not help that target. Use the ordinary player-roll procedure,
without a second opposed save.

## Make and identify items

A recipe tells you what you can make, what it takes and how you learn it. Trail
Workshop I teaches two recipes from its list. Trail Workshop II teaches two
more. Teachers and discoveries can provide other recipes.

Gather the ingredients and complete the recipe's work interval on your own.
Roll Knowledge when called for and apply progress or inventory steps as the
recipe describes. A supply starts at d4 and grows up to the recipe's cap. Check
the inventory die for any carried materials you used.

Inspect an unfamiliar item to learn what its appearance and ordinary use reveal.
If the item requires deeper understanding, follow its instructions. The Identify
spell in the [Detection spellbook](../../spellbooks/detection.md) reveals a magical
item's properties as described on its page.

## Rest and development

The DA determines whether a place is safe enough to spend saved DP. Level awards
are milestones, not payment for each defeated creature. Record your learned abilities, recipes, remaining DP and scars.

First Aid uses supplies and time; safe overnight rest restores HP and SP.
An inn, a friendly workshop, a healer's garden or a camp among allies can become part
of the story. Gather supplies before you leave, and note what the company still needs to replace.
