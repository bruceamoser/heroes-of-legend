# Harm and Recovery

You can catch your breath after a battle. You cannot always leave the battle behind.
HP measures your immediate capacity to keep going. Grit gives you a limited reserve
when you fall. Wounds record the lasting cost, even when their active penalties
have been treated.

## HP, healing and a safe breath

Subtract damage from current HP. Healing restores its printed amount, after any
Life resistance, up to maximum HP. When the immediate danger has passed and you
can safely take a breath, restore HP to full. The DA determines whether that pause
is safe. A breath does not erase a wound or replenish spent Grit.

Deal with immediate conditions as the fiction permits. Companions can put out an
ordinary fire when no danger prevents them from doing so. Poison, persistent injuries
and other unresolved conditions do not all disappear because combat ended. Use each
condition's removal rule. Resolve a Dying character's situation before leaving turn timing.

## When you fall

Player characters enter **Dying** at 0 HP. Each entry causes a new wound. A creature
or ordinary NPC normally dies at 0 HP; player-character survival procedures are not
a second HP pool for every opponent.

Roll the new wound immediately. Roll three d6, plus one extra die for each **prior
wound**, to a maximum of **five dice total**. Retain the highest three, sort them from
lowest to highest, and read them as a three-digit number. Add the new scar to your
wound count after determining this roll.

> **Example — a second scar.** You already have one wound, so roll four dice for
> the next one. On 2, 4, 4, 6, keep 4, 4, 6: result 446. Read 446 on the appropriate
> wound table. It is not a total of fourteen. Even after treatment, your earlier scar
> still adds its die to this roll.

Choose the table for the damage type that dealt the most damage **after resistance**.
You choose on a tie. Physical includes sonic and force. Fire, Cold and Lightning use
Elemental wounds, but keep separate resistances. Mental, Radiant and Life/Necrotic
have their own tables.

> **Fully resisted components.** If an event dealt only its minimum
> 1 total damage because all components were reduced to zero, choose a wound table
> from the event's original damage types. The player chooses which one applies.

If the listed body part is absent or the injury cannot apply, move to the next
listed result until one does, such as 116 to 122. Do not reroll for a more convenient
injury. The tables end at 666.

## Your Dying turn

At the start of your turn while Dying, choose **one Grit** or a **death roll**. This
choice stands for the turn; you cannot see the roll and then decide to spend Grit.

Spend one Grit to regain full HP. You retain the wound you just suffered. Otherwise,
roll Fortitude and apply the death-roll result.

| Death-roll result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Weak | Gain 1 death mark. |
| Standard | No change. |
| Strong | Stabilize at 0 HP and clear death marks. |
| Retained natural 111 | Gain 2 death marks instead of the ordinary Weak result. |
| Retained natural 666 | Restore 1 HP and clear death marks. |

At **three death marks**, enter Last Words. Damage while already Dying adds one
mark per damaging event; it does not create another wound. Damage that returns a
stabilized character to Dying creates a new wound and adds one death mark.

A companion can use the universal **Stabilize** Maneuver. Stabilization clears death
marks and leaves you at 0 HP, unable to act. Healing above 0 clears death marks and
ends HP-caused unconsciousness. It does not necessarily remove another condition
that also prevents action.

## Last Words

A wound result of **666**, or reaching three death marks, begins **Last Words**. You
can speak, but do nothing else. You die at the end of the second following turn.
Use that time for a farewell, a warning, a promise or silence. It belongs to the player.

Ordinary healing, treatment, potions and Grit cannot reverse Last Words. Only an
explicit exceptional ability could say otherwise. Another character's ability may
react to hearing those words without changing the finality of your death.

## Treating a wound

A wound states its immediate effect, any lasting penalty, its required treatment
and **Recovery Value**, or **RV**. Immediate pushes and knockdowns happen once.
Temporary conditions decay under the condition rules. Lasting wound penalties
remain until their stated remedy.

Use the appropriate treatment card: Soft Tissue, Bone, Mental, Elemental, Radiant
or Life. Each attempt takes the card's full time. A wound can receive only **one
treatment attempt per day**, across all healers. Weak, Standard and Strong add
**1, 2 and 3 recovery progress**. Keep accumulated progress with the wound.

When progress meets or exceeds RV, remove exactly the effects its remedy says it
removes. The scar and accumulated wound count remain. Treatment does not regrow a
missing limb. An artificial replacement or exceptional ability specifies the
function it restores. Rest works as a remedy only when the wound explicitly lists it.

> **Example — two different problems.** Physical wound 333 applies Bleeding 1 and
> a lasting Bane. Bleeding resolves and decays, or can be reduced with an appropriate
> Main Action. It does not restart each turn because the scar exists. Soft Tissue
> treatment must accumulate RV 3 to remove the lasting Bane. The scar still counts
> on every later wound roll.

A cosmetic wound can need no treatment. If treating a socially significant scar
removes its intimidation Boon and persuasion Bane, remove **both**, as printed.
The DA decides when broad physical or mental activity penalties apply.

## Temporary and lasting conditions

Read the condition's source as well as its name. An ability, wound or other effect
says how the condition begins and which remedy ends it. A rating, an asterisk and
a wound's Recovery Value tell you different things.

| What you record | What time does | How it ends |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary numbered condition, such as Bleeding 3 or Blinded 2 | At the end of your turn, resolve its effect, then reduce its temporary rating by 1 | Reaching 0, or a remedy that applies to that condition |
| Starred condition, such as Blinded 1* | The starred rating stays until its remedy applies | The source's specified treatment, replacement or other remedy |
| Lasting wound penalty, such as a Bane or movement loss | The penalty stays until its printed remedy applies | Complete that wound's treatment or another remedy the wound lists |
| Maintained or binary state, such as Grappled or Prone | Follow that state's own rules | Release or escape the grapple; spend 2 movement to stand from Prone |

**Temporary** means the numbered contribution decays automatically. A condition
gained during your turn also decreases at that turn's end. The number may measure
severity, duration, or both: Blinded 2 prevents sight until it clears, while Dazed
can restrict fewer action categories as its rating falls.

**Lasting** means the effect stays until its remedy. An asterisk marks no automatic
decay. It does not mean incurable: Blinded 1* can be treatable when its source says
so. Some injuries cause permanent loss; those entries identify replacement or an
exceptional remedy rather than ordinary treatment. Rest restores HP when safe;
a lasting condition follows its own remedy.

### Removing a lasting condition

1. **Find its source.** Read the ability or wound that applied it. For a wound,
   record its table and result, lasting effects, Treatment / remedy and RV.
2. **Use the listed remedy.** A treatment label identifies the required capability:
   Soft Tissue, Bone, Mental, Elemental, Radiant or Life. A replacement or special
   cure instead follows its own procedure. A dash in RV means there is no treatment
   progress track; read the remedy column.
3. **Track recovery where an RV is printed.** Use the matching treatment ability
   and its full time. One wound receives one attempt per day across all healers.
   Add 1, 2 or 3 progress for Weak, Standard or Strong and keep that progress.
4. **Remove the effects the remedy names.** Reaching RV completes that wound's
   treatment. The scar still counts toward future wound rolls. A replacement
   restores only the function its remedy specifies.

> **Example: two kinds of blindness.** Blinded 2 from a temporary effect clears
> after two end-of-turn decay steps. Physical wound 366 instead causes Blinded 1*
> and lists Soft Tissue treatment, RV 5. A Standard treatment adds 2 progress; a
> Strong treatment on a later day adds 3. At 5, sight returns. The scar remains.

### When temporary and lasting effects overlap

Record temporary and starred contributions separately. Add effective severity
where the condition uses severity, but decay only the temporary part. For a
duration-only condition, either positive contribution keeps the condition active.
An ability that reduces temporary Dazed affects that temporary contribution;
lasting treatment follows its source.

If an eye injury already causes Blinded 1* and dust adds temporary Blinded 2,
clearing the dust leaves the wound's blindness in place. Treating the eye injury
resolves its starred contribution; any remaining temporary blindness follows its
own decay or remedy.

Dazed and Grappled determine your retained action categories at **turn start**.
A change during the turn does not undo actions already spent or recalculate that
turn's category selection. Grappled still prevents movement while the hold exists.
Paralyzed, Asleep and Unconscious impose their immediate restrictions.

You can spend a Main Action to reduce Bleeding, Burning or Freezing on yourself or
an adjacent creature by 1 with a plausible method. This is not a cure for every
condition. Specialized cards may remove more or use a cheaper action.

> **Example — resolve, then decay.** Untreated Bleeding 3 deals 3 damage at turn end,
> then becomes Bleeding 2. On later turns it deals 2 and then 1 before ending.
> Reducing it with a Main Action before the first tick changes the sequence to 2,
> then 1. Bleeding bypasses resistance; Burning and Freezing use matching resistance.

Asleep, Paralyzed, Unconscious and Petrified impose three defense Banes. Overlapping
helpless states apply that penalty once. Dazed adds no defense Banes. Grappled gives
attackers one Boon per rating of the actual target. When Dazed and Grappled overlap,
use the greater action restriction, not their sum.
