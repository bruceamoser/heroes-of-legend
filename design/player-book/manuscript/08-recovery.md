# Harm and Recovery

Your HP measures how much danger you can endure before you fall. Rest, treatment
and magic restore it. Grit is the courage that brings you back when ordinary
endurance fails. Scars remember what happened after the numbers recover.

## HP and growing endurance

At level 0, maximum HP is **10 + ancestry HP contribution + Fortitude + explicit
HP benefits**. At level 1, add your class HP contribution. At every level after
1, add your ancestry and class HP contributions again. Each contribution is 1,
2 or 3; record their sum as your HP gain per level. Fortitude and fixed benefits
are added once, not multiplied by level.

For level 1 or higher: **maximum HP = 10 + level × (ancestry + class HP
contributions) + Fortitude + explicit HP benefits**. On gaining a level, increase
current HP by the same amount as maximum HP; this does not end Dying or restore
HP to a character at 0. Such a character gains the maximum only until recovered.

Damage reduces current HP, to a minimum of 0. Healing restores the stated amount,
up to maximum HP. Apply Life resistance to magical healing and potions, with a
minimum of 1 actual healing when HP is missing. First aid and rest are not Life
magic and ignore Life resistance. Healing cannot restore the dead.

## Falling to 0 HP

A player character reduced from positive HP to 0 enters **Dying**, falls prone and
becomes unconscious. Clear old death marks when a new Dying episode begins. There
is no wound roll or negative HP total. Ordinary NPCs and creatures normally die
at 0; summoned forms disappear.

At the start of each Dying turn, make a **Fortitude death roll** using the normal
3d6, Boons and Banes. You can spend Grit before rolling or immediately after a
result adds death marks, including the third mark.

| Death-roll result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Weak | Add 1 death mark. |
| Standard | No change. |
| Strong | Stabilize at 0 HP and clear all death marks. |
| Retained natural 111 | Add 2 death marks instead of the ordinary Weak result. |
| Retained natural 666 | Restore 1 HP, clear death marks and end Dying. |

At **three death marks**, spend Grit immediately or die. A farewell belongs to
the player, but grants no extra rounds or actions. A printed ability that prevents
death must resolve at its stated trigger, before death is final.

### Further damage

Every damaging event while already at 0 HP causes **one immediate death roll**.
This replaces automatic death marks from damage. Do not add a separate mark for
the hit or one roll per damage component. Separate events, including later
Bleeding or Burning damage, each cause their own roll. Damage while stabilized
first returns you to Dying, then causes this immediate roll. The event that first
reduces positive HP to 0 does not also cause an immediate death roll.

Resolve the damage and immediate death roll before moving on to the next event.
If healing or Grit restores positive HP, later damage reduces that HP normally.
Keep turn timing until Dying and immediate hazards have been resolved; ending a
battle does not skip death rolls.

## Stabilization and recovery at 0

Anyone can use the **Stabilize Maneuver** on an adjacent Dying hero, without a roll
or supplies. Clear their death marks and end Dying, leaving them at **0 HP**. They
regain consciousness from HP loss, but remain prone and cannot take Main Actions,
Maneuvers, Reactions or voluntary movement until they regain HP. Apply the same three defense Banes as Unconscious while at 0. They can speak,
perceive their surroundings and accept help. Companions can carry them. Another
source of unconsciousness or incapacity still follows its own remedy.

A stabilized hero makes no turn-start death rolls. Further damage restarts Dying.
Healing above 0 ends Dying, clears marks and ends HP-caused unconsciousness; it
does not automatically stand the hero up or remove other conditions. Healing may
target a Dying or stabilized hero without requiring consent or a Reaction.

## Grit: three chances across a career

Gain **1 Grit at level 1**, **1 more at level 3**, and **1 more at level 7**. Record
each grant and each expenditure. Grit never refreshes through rest, treatment,
magic or a new session. Unspent points carry forward; spending one removes it
permanently. These milestones are the only Grit grants.

Spend one Grit when you fall to 0, at the start of a Dying turn, immediately after
a death roll adds marks, or while stabilized at 0. This costs no action or Reaction.
Set current HP to **half maximum HP, rounded up**, clear death marks, and end Dying
and HP-caused unconsciousness. Other conditions remain. You may spend Grit after
the roll that would give your third mark, before death becomes final. You cannot
spend it after death. Grit never raises you above an explicit recovery ceiling.

> **Example: the third strike.** With 17 maximum HP and two death marks, you roll
> Weak. You spend your last Grit immediately: set HP to 9 and clear the marks.
> Without Grit or an ability that prevents this death, the third mark kills you.

## Scars tell the story

Surviving an incident in which you reached 0 HP leaves a permanent descriptive
scar. Choose it with the DA once the danger has passed: a pale seam, singed hair,
a shaking hand when thunder sounds, or a remembered voice. One incident can include
several falls and rescues; it leaves one scar, rather than a new entry for every
hit at 0. A later incident can add another.

A scar changes no attribute, HP, movement, Boons, Banes or future death rolls. It
cannot require a treatment track or remove a body part. Its emotional meaning
belongs to the player. Treatment and healing restore capability without erasing
history. See [Scars and Survival](../../rules/wounds.md) for prompts.

## Safe overnight rest

After **eight hours in a place the DA judges safe**, with food, water and adequate
shelter, restore full HP and SP and renew your first-aid allowance. Include at least
six hours of sleep or equivalent rest; the other two can include quiet activity
or a watch. Oath-Bound use the same rest period, with stillness in place of sleep.
You can gain these benefits only once in 24 hours.

A fight, strenuous work or forced travel interrupts the rest; complete a new
uninterrupted period afterward. Brief waking, conversation and routine watch do
not. The DA explains what makes a campsite unsafe before the group commits to it.
A stabilized hero can recover this way; a Dying hero must first be stabilized.

A few quiet minutes do not restore HP or refresh abilities. There is no short-rest
reset. Rest does not restore Grit, erase scars or remove conditions whose remedies
say otherwise. A specific recovery ability can improve rest without changing these
shared limits.

## First aid and treatment

Recovery uses time, a daily treatment allowance and SP for magic. There is no Hit Dice pool.

Stabilization is the immediate rescue. **First Aid** is slower care that anyone can
provide: spend **ten uninterrupted minutes**, use one supply of clean dressings
and medicine, and make a **Knowledge roll** for yourself or one willing, Dying or
stabilized living creature within reach. Restore **2/4/6 HP** on Weak/Standard/Strong.
Resolve the supply's inventory die after use. No supply means no attempt. Treatment does not suspend Dying or its rolls; stabilize a fallen patient first when possible.

Each recipient can receive **one treatment attempt between safe overnight rests**,
across all helpers and treatment abilities. Mark the allowance when the attempt
begins; an interrupted attempt consumes it and the supply but restores no HP.
Another injury, healer or treatment type does not create a new allowance.
Stabilizing someone and reducing conditions do not consume this allowance.

Learned treatment improves this same attempt or adds a specific benefit. It never
adds another attempt unless its text explicitly says so. Ordinary treatment does
not remove curses, regrow limbs or cure a starred condition without a named remedy.

## Magical healing and spell points

Your SP reserve is separate from HP. Add **1 SP from ancestry, 1 from culture,
1 from background and 3 from class**: 3 maximum SP at level 0, 6 at level 1, before
explicit improvements. These contributions are added once. Learning magic is
separate from having SP. Safe overnight rest restores spent SP; brief pauses do not.

Healing magic spends the caster's printed **SP cost before resolution**, whether
used in battle or during quiet time. You must have the SP to pay. It does not cost
HP. A free hand and the printed range still apply. Healing can be repeated while
SP remains; it does not consume a recipient's first-aid allowance. A potion instead
uses its printed healing and inventory die. Neither replenishes Grit or SP.

A summoned healer's controller pays the SP cost. Summoned energy forms cannot
receive healing and disappear at 0 HP. No ability may turn HP healing into SP
recovery unless it explicitly supplies that conversion.

## Conditions keep their own rules

At turn end, resolve a temporary numbered condition, then reduce its rating by 1.
A starred contribution has no automatic decay; use its source's remedy. Keep
starred and temporary contributions separate. Scars never apply conditions.

Spend a Main Action and use a plausible method to reduce Bleeding, Burning or
Freezing on yourself or an adjacent creature by 1, without a roll. This does not
heal HP or consume first aid. Other conditions use their own removal procedures.
A curse can persist through an overnight rest even though the hero's HP returns.
