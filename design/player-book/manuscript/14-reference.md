# Table References

Use these pages when the action pauses for a rule. Keep your most-used abilities
beside your sheet, along with the conditions currently affecting you. The alphabetical
index includes universal actions, shared abilities and class signatures.

## Your turn in order

1. Resolve any start-of-turn instructions. If Dying, choose Grit or a death roll.
2. Refresh your turn budget. If Dazed or Grappled, choose the action categories you retain.
3. Move and take permitted actions in your chosen order. Declare an ability before rolling.
4. Resolve end-of-turn conditions, then reduce temporary numbered ratings by 1.
5. Mark continuing effects and keep your available Reaction ready for its trigger.

## Quick rules

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Ordinary roll | 3d6 + one attribute; Weak ≤8, Standard 9–14, Strong ≥15 |
| Boons / Banes | Cancel; add up to 3 net dice; keep highest / lowest 3 |
| Natural 111 / 666 | Weak + complication / Strong + benefit, using retained dice |
| Initiative | One unmodified 3d6; 12+ players first; same side begins each round |
| Action conversion | Main → Maneuver → movement; never upward |
| Ordinary movement | Normal 1; difficult 2; diagonal 1; stand costs 2 |
| Occupied space | Cross only if occupant Prone, cost 2; never end there |
| Cover | Partial 1 Bane; heavy 3 Banes; no path means no attack |
| Resistance | Highest matching type, cap 3; minimum 1 damage per event |
| Stability | Default 0; highest explicit grant, cap 3; deduct once per new creature |
| Free attacks | Spend no actions; never trigger more free attacks |
| Healing spell cost | Novice 1, Adept 3, Master 7 HP; pay first, stay above 0 |
| Potion | Free hand, Maneuver; another recipient uses Reaction unless at 0 HP |
| Safe breath | Full HP; Grit and wounds remain |
| Wound treatment | One attempt per wound per day; 1/2/3 progress toward RV |
| Supplies | Roll inventory die after use; on 1, step down; last use works |

## Glossary

**Ability.** A learned technique, spell, talent or other capability with its own
procedure. “Card” refers to that rules entry, whether printed on a card or in this book.

**Action.** Something a character chooses to do. An action can call for a roll;
a roll itself is not an extra action. A Main Action is a specific part of the turn budget.

**Attribute.** One of Brawn, Fortitude, Agility, Guile, Knowledge or Reason. Add only
the attribute called for by the action or DA.

**Benefit.** A useful additional advantage, including the extra consequence of a
natural 666. Apply the stated Strong outcome as well.

**Boon / Bane.** An extra die that changes which three dice you retain. Boons and
Banes cancel before applying their net cap.

**Bulk.** An item's inventory load. Capacity and carried Bulk determine encumbrance.

**Class.** Your signature identity and discipline pricing. Choose one; there is no multiclassing.

**Complication.** An additional problem that does not erase progress already earned.

**Condition.** A named state with defined effects and removal. A condition's rating
may measure severity, duration or both.

**Consequence.** What is at stake in an uncertain attempt. Establish it before rolling.

**DA.** Dungeon Architect, the participant who presents and adjudicates the world.

**Discipline.** A ranked field of study used to qualify for learned abilities. Rank
alone grants no bonus or action.

**DP.** Development Points, spent on eligible ranks and abilities. Level-0 and level-1
pools expire separately; later awards may be saved.

**DR.** Damage reduction. Armor DR is physical resistance, applied after the attack outcome.

**Dying.** A player character's state at 0 HP before stabilization or recovery.
Entering it causes a wound; repeated damage while already Dying adds death marks.

**Effect.** A change produced by a rule. It might be immediate damage, movement,
a condition or a continuing benefit. Its duration does not repeat instant damage.

**Evasion / Threat.** A creature's printed dice adjustments to player attacks /
player defenses. They are Boons or Banes, not added numerical totals.

**Free attack.** An attack granted by a specific rule without spending an action.
It cannot trigger another free attack.

**Grit.** A limited reserve that can restore a Dying hero to full HP. Ordinary rest
does not replenish it; the wound remains.

**Hidden.** A universal concealed state, maintained by actual circumstances. Attacking
or openly revealing yourself ends it.

**HP.** Current capacity to keep going. Record current and maximum separately.

**Inventory die.** A supply's remaining uncertainty. Roll after use; step down on 1.

**Last Words.** Speech only, then death at the end of the second following turn.
Ordinary healing, potions, treatment and Grit cannot reverse it.

**Maneuver.** A smaller action used for movement techniques, setup, support and practical tasks.

**Passive.** A capability that requires no action unless its text says otherwise.

**Reaction.** Your limited response to a stated trigger. An ordinary defense roll
is not itself a Reaction.

**Recovery Value.** The accumulated treatment progress needed to remove the wound's
listed lasting effects. Scar count remains after recovery.

**Resistance.** Reduction to a matching damage type; highest source, maximum 3.
Life resistance also reduces ordinary healing.

**Safe place.** A location the DA judges suitable for spending saved DP. A safe breath
is the shorter recovery pause that restores HP when immediate danger is resolved.

**Signature.** A class-exclusive ability. Its prerequisites are class and minimum level,
not discipline ranks or earlier signatures.

**Stability.** Resistance to the distance of forced movement. Default 0, cap 3.
It does not reduce damage, Prone or other conditions.

**Tier.** Novice, Adept or Master, with minimum levels 1, 3 and 7 and prices 2, 4 and 8 DP.

**Wound.** A lasting injury record caused by entering Dying. Its active penalty may
be treated; its scar continues to count for later wound rolls.

## Record useful playtest feedback

Write down what happened, which abilities were used, and what slowed the table.
Note a confusing phrase as well as the ruling you made. Record striking moments:
a rescue, a clever use of terrain, an ability that never found a useful opportunity.
Those observations help improve the game more than a bare claim that something is
strong or weak.
