# Card conventions

These conventions apply to the current catalog.

## Learn, declare, resolve

Each nonmagical path teaches an ability I, then II and III improve that same ability. Those effects add together unless a new total replaces an earlier total. The final level-3 entry teaches a distinct ability. Every hero can make basic melee and ranged attacks. Arms and Marksmanship develop those fighting skills further.

A magical discipline opens four spellbooks. Each book's first section teaches pages 1–2, the second pages 3–4, the third pages 5–6, and the level-3 endpoint pages 7–8 plus all three pages of its advanced book. Learn sections for 1/2/2/3 DP. Page 1 costs 0 SP to cast; it still must be learned. Spellcraft instead provides Soul Capacity, Mind Capacity, Recovery and the Channeling spellbook. Capacity increases add to one shared SP pool.

Combined abilities require level 7, every named branch endpoint and a separate learning cost: 3 DP for two disciplines, 4 DP for three, 5 DP for four. Magical and nonmagical disciplines may combine. Owning only a discipline or a partially learned branch does not satisfy an endpoint. No lower-count combined ability is required unless explicitly listed.

The six independent 1-DP talents remain available once each: Another Tongue, Message by Heart, Catch That!, A Party Piece, Quick Exit and Look Over Here! Class signatures are gained at their milestones, not bought as additional independent abilities.

Declare a card before rolling. Roll 3d6 plus its one printed attribute with the
normal Boon/Bane rules. No skill bonus, weapon damage die or extra attribute damage.
A no-roll card simply does what it says. Rolled cards give their Weak, Standard
and Strong results in the text; a shared 1/2/3 progression always follows that order.

Range uses squares. “Adjacent” means within one square with an actual accessible
path. Visible targets still require an attack path. A player area/multitarget
attack uses one roll with the worst applicable net Boon/Bane situation. NPC attacks
give each affected player a defense roll. A printed NPC defense attribute is a
choice for that attack, not the attribute a player attacker rolls.

## Costs and time

Main Action, Maneuver, Reaction, Movement and Passive are distinct labels. A long
activity prints its full time; “Main Action” does not allow an hour of treatment in
one turn. No-roll does not mean free. A triggered Passive effect spends no action
unless stated. A Reaction spends the one available Reaction.

Instant damage or displacement occurs once. A named temporary condition continues
under the condition rules, not forever because the attack’s duration is Instant.
“Once per turn” means your turn unless a different creature’s turn is named.
“Once per round” resets at the start of your next turn.
“Scene” means one continuous situation; a brief pause does not reset it.

Healing costs 1/2/4 SP by tier, paid before the roll; the caster must have the SP. Requires a free hand. Healing resistance is Life resistance;
minimum 1 applies only when HP is missing. No healing above maximum. Potions use
their own fixed value and inventory die, with no spell outcome roll or caster cost.

## Equipment and grants

A grant supplies a missing ordinary item on acquisition. It does not supply another
compatible weapon just because another attack was learned, and selling the item
does not reactivate a past grant. Ordinary melee and ranged weapons each use Bulk 1.
Their names and appearance add no damage. Special equipment adds only printed effects.
A requirement without a grant may require treasure or coins; flag that in starter advice.

## Bonus and effect language

Same-kind numeric bonuses use the highest. Boons/Banes stack normally. Numbered
conditions stack where meaningful; repeated binary Prone does not become Prone 2.
Free attacks never trigger more free attacks. A free attack does not consume actions,
but only the granting effect determines what attack is permitted.

Displacements use the shared movement rules. “Move without provoking” does not allow
walking through walls or occupied standing creatures. Teleportation is explicit.
Flavor never silently grants flight, telepathy, armor, a second damage type or immunity.

## Proposed sorting types

Attack, Defense, Movement, Control, Recovery, Utility, Skill, Magic, Crafting, Training and
Signature are tags, not additional rules. Multiple tags are allowed. Action cost is
indexed separately. Ancestry, Origin, Discipline, Equipment and Recipe identify
record kinds rather than learned-card prerequisites.

Printed layout later: numbered diamond top left, name top center, discipline icons
top right. Type, action, range, outcomes, duration and limits remain visible. Color
can support the tier but may never replace its number.

## A different kind of expertise

Use the attribute printed on the ability. Learning an unusual attack does not replace every attack’s attribute. No skill modifier is added.

Beast forms keep your own HP, scars and attributes; they are not disposable HP
pools. Use the form’s printed actions and equipment restrictions. The new cards
state how to enter and leave a form, so the table does not need a monster stat block.

Noncombat cards should be read as things a person can do. Correct ordinary action
still needs no roll when no consequence is at stake. An expert adds an opportunity,
useful detail or reliability; owning the card is not a license for everyone else to
stop thinking creatively.

## Imagine it, then resolve it

**Agreed October 13, 2026.** Every card begins with narrative text describing what
using it looks, sounds or feels like. One vivid sentence is enough for a simple
ability; a more elaborate ability may deserve more. Address the player and make
an action or experience imaginable. This applies to universal actions, signatures,
shared abilities and the other character, equipment and recipe records.

Follow the narrative with a distinct **Rules** section. Only this section establishes
mechanical permissions and effects. State who acts, the target, action or full time,
attribute or no-roll instruction, prerequisites, costs, outcomes, duration and limits
as applicable. A triggered effect states its trigger and resolution timing. A movement
effect states its distance and legal endpoints. A benefit states its recipients and
expiry. Do not bury any of these in the narrative.

For example, “You tie the knot without looking, then test it with a tug” evokes
experience. “While climbing, secure one held rope at a reachable suitable anchor
without a separate action; a free hand is required” is the adjudicated rule.
Narrative does not imply an extra sense, free item, automatic success or magical power.

Keep acquisition facts in Rules too. An origin’s memory prompt belongs above Rules;
its paired discipline/card grant belongs below. For an item, describe the experience
of handling it, then specify its actual additive effects and use requirements.

## Development breadth

Each discipline has four sequential paths. The first three learned entries build one ability; the endpoint adds a new capability at level 3. Combined abilities require every exact endpoint, level 7 and 3/4/5 DP for two/three/four disciplines. Class signatures keep class-and-level requirements. See [learning](rules/learning.md).

## Concrete language review

**Bruce’s correction, October 13, 2026:** a narrative heading and an evocative slogan
are insufficient. Describe what the character actually does and what becomes visible,
audible or felt. “You reach for tomorrow’s sky” leaves the action unspecified. Reaching
through a tear in the air, pulling out a clump of weather and tossing it onto the
ground gives the player an action to imagine. Passive expertise may instead describe
the practiced habit or observation that produces its benefit.

Read the narrative independently of the title and rules. Could a player describe
that action to the table? Does it suggest the effect this card actually supplies?
Avoid replacing a specific action with a promise, maxim or mysterious metaphor.
Sensory detail should make the action clearer; it should not imply an extra power.

Read the rules in the order someone uses them: choose the target, meet requirements,
pay the action/cost, make any roll, apply the result, then track its expiry. Explain
unusual dice operations on the card. “Lowest retained die” is not sufficient: say
which dice are examined after Boons/Banes and whether the printed numbers or modified
total matter. Put separate effects in separate sentences or bullets. Show Weak,
Standard and Strong rather than compressing a whole procedure into unexplained slashes.

Finally, read every field against the card’s purpose. A swimming card must not carry
a recipient treatment limit; an area card should say which creatures it affects rather
than recite rules for unrelated target shapes. Structural checks cannot perform this
editorial review, and passing them must not be presented as proof that wording is clear.

## Table and VTT resolution

**Review revision, October 13, 2026.** Read a card in this order: check permission
and equipment, declare choices and targets, pay its action and costs, roll if called
for, apply the complete outcome, then mark its duration. Choices of damage type
are made before rolling; no ability in this revision inspects an individual die to
select a second unrelated effect.

A card's full activity time overrides its combat action label. Crafting and using
a crafted supply are separate procedures. Do not reduce an hour of work to a Main
Action because the action field uses that label for sorting.

Outcome bullets are complete results, not cumulative instructions: apply only the
rolled bullet. Where it says “also” or “both benefits,” apply those stated benefits
as well. Instant damage happens once; numbered conditions then follow their own
resolve-and-decay rules. A duration field never makes attack damage recur.

For an area described as “within N squares,” every selected square must be in
range. A connected placed area uses edge-sharing squares. Rectangles extend in a
chosen grid direction from a square adjacent to the caster; choose their footprint
before rolling. Include allies only where the card targets every creature. Resolve
multiple displacements in an order the acting player chooses, using the shared
collision rules; unresolved moving-group geometry remains a DA ruling.

Use Push for movement directly away from the source, Pull for movement directly
toward it, and Slide for movement in any direction. State the maximum distance in
squares and print any special source point, path or destination restrictions.

Use the character's existing roll for a reaction that modifies that roll. A card
which grants Basic Melee directs its recipient to the Basic Melee card; “no separate
roll” never makes the granted attack automatic Strong. A player attack still gets
one roll and never causes a second player defense roll.

A Boon to defense against forced movement applies to an attack's ordinary defense
if that attack can move the defender. It never creates an extra defense against
no-roll displacement. Cards that depend on a defense roll do nothing when the
normal player-attacker rule supplies no such roll.

VTT records should store action, full time, attribute or delegated roll, targets,
range, shape, choices, outcomes, equipment, cost, condition rating, duration anchor,
and use limit separately. Track a continuing benefit as one effect with its stated
expiry, not a fresh bonus every time the token is selected. Narrative, physical
plausibility, willing participation and DA judgments stay visible to the people
playing; do not turn them into extra automated saves.

## Shared access and condition combinations

Every class can learn a shared ability by meeting its printed level and discipline
requirements and paying its price. Discipline access costs 1 DP for every class. Class signatures retain their printed class requirements.

An ability that targets every creature meeting a condition affects the whole
eligible group within its printed range and shape. The condition remains unless
the ability explicitly changes or removes it. Player-selected activation order
supports one character setting up another character's payoff; normal condition
timing continues to apply.

## Class signature standard

**Agreed:** signatures define their class and are slightly more powerful than shared
cards at the same tier. Give each choice a useful, recognizable advantage: better
action efficiency, reliability, scope, positioning or a distinctive permission.
A vivid name alone does not establish that advantage. Compare the whole procedure,
including costs, targets and limits, rather than only damage.

Each milestone offers independent choices. Require only class and minimum level;
never require an earlier signature. All learned signatures remain available, so
review combinations of purchased passives as well as individual cards.

Signature records retain Type: Signature
and use Tags for their functional roles; action cost remains a separate field.

## Focused Maneuver development

**Agreed direction:** use Maneuvers for special positioning, setup and support.
Keep ordinary movement simple; do not introduce movement-priced ability activations
or add variable-cost/conversion cards merely to fill a catalog category. Independent options span all three tiers.

## Stability and movement

All pushes, pulls and throws of creatures use [Stability](rules/stability.md),
including additional creatures struck in a collision chain. Distances on cards are
before Stability. Other riders still apply when displacement is reduced to 0.
Voluntary movement, projectile range, obstacle progress and grapple reduction are
not reduced by Stability. Any exception must be explicit on the card.

## Approved playtest catalog, November 1, 2026

The earlier independent Adept and Master catalog has been replaced by sequential discipline paths and level-7 combined abilities. Class signatures are milestone choices, not additional DP purchases.
Ability entries omit empty Duration, Equipment and Limits fields; any specific
expiry, equipment or use limit remains in the rules or its printed field.
