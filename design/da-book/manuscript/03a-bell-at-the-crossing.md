# The Bell at the Crossing

A bell sounds across a misty river. Each time it rings, the ferryman pulls his boat
farther onto the bank. The travelers stranded at his shelter have begun arguing
about the old causeway. Nobody agrees on what the signal means.

This short adventure suits a company beginning at level 1. Its possible fights
use existing creature cards and provisional CV totals; those totals are not a
promise of difficulty. Read the party's actual capabilities before play. The
adventure can be resolved without fighting, and its locations can occupy one
session or expand into several.

**New adventure content:** names, placements, rewards and local obstacle targets
are manuscript proposals for review. They do not establish universal rules.

## What happened

The far landing stands above an older guard station. Two salvage workers opened
its sealed signal room and disturbed three Skeleton Sentries whose final command
was to keep strangers beyond the painted threshold. One worker, Nera, escaped
into the bell loft. Her companion Tovin is hiding in a stores alcove outside that
threshold. Both are alive when the adventure begins.

A small bandit crew has occupied the toll yard above the landing. They claim that
the bell signals a deadly plague and demand payment for passage along the causeway.
Their leader, Brin, knows there is no plague. Brin has heard the sentries but has
not entered their chamber. The crew wants money and a way out before the district's
road wardens arrive.

The ferryman, Oren, knows the bell's old rescue signal. What he hears is close to
that signal but irregular. He fears rowing passengers into an ambush. Mara, a
carrier with goods stranded across the river, offers the company **80 coins total**
to restore safe access and bring any survivors out. The agreement does not require
them to kill anyone. Food and a dry place in Oren's shelter are available while
they investigate.

## People to portray

| Person | What the heroes notice | Want and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Oren, ferryman | Checks the boat's knots whenever the bell sounds. | Wants proof that the landing is safe; will share his knowledge before risking passengers. |
| Mara, carrier | Keeps a careful list of everyone stranded across the river. | Wants people and goods recovered; can pay the promised 80 coins, not an unlimited ransom. |
| Brin, toll thief | Speaks confidently until asked who issued the quarantine. | Wants supplies and escape; will abandon the toll if exposed with a safe way to leave. |
| Nera, salvager | Rings the bell with a broken spar and shouts between peals. | Wants Tovin rescued; can describe the sentries' boundary. |
| Tovin, salvager | Hides beneath empty sacks, clutching the station's keeper log. | Wants a safe route out; knows the west service hatch avoids the threshold. |

None of these people needs to become a fight merely because the heroes distrust
them. If a consequential NPC-only action needs a roll, use the ordinary NPC
procedure; Oren's description does not grant an unprinted combat ability.

## Routes at a glance

| From | To | Route and information |
|---|---|---|
| Ferry shelter | Toll yard | A visible causeway; the crew can see travelers approaching. |
| Ferry shelter | Far landing | Oren's boat, once convinced; no automatic travel roll on calm water. |
| Causeway | West service hatch | A bank path found by examining the old maintenance marker; reeds conceal it from the yard. |
| Toll yard | Bell loft | Exterior stairs; the crew avoids the loft because of the noise below. |
| Far landing | Signal room entrance | A short stone passage ending at a clearly painted boundary. |
| Service hatch | Stores alcove | A cramped but passable maintenance corridor, outside the sentries' assigned boundary. |
| Bell loft | Signal room entrance | Interior stairs; Nera can warn approaching heroes from above. |

Use ordinary travel narration between these nearby places unless pursuit or another
immediate danger makes movement order important. No route is secretly impassable
just because it avoids a prepared encounter.

## 1. The ferry shelter

Describe a rain-dark roof, travelers sharing a small fire, and the bell beyond the
mist. Give the players Mara's offer and Oren's explanation. Examining the signal
chart nailed beside his door reveals that repeated paired strokes request rescue.
The present strokes are broken by long pauses, not a quarantine signal.

Oren will lend the boat and guide the crossing if the heroes show him a credible
plan for checking the landing. He can be convinced by the chart and a proposal to
approach away from the yard; a roll is unnecessary once his concern is answered.
An uncertain attempt to persuade him to row straight into danger uses the social
procedure, with its stakes agreed first. Taking the boat without consent changes
his relationship to the party rather than automatically starting a battle.

The maintenance marker on the causeway map is visible to anyone who examines it.
Oren remembers that workers once used a path beneath the western bank. This gives
the party another route even if nobody can negotiate boat passage.

## 2. The false toll

Use [three Road Bandits](../../bestiary/creatures/road-bandit.md) and
[one Bandit Bow](../../bestiary/creatures/bandit-bow.md), **CV 36 total**.
Brin uses one Road Bandit card, with no additional leader actions.

For a grid, draw a yard **12 squares wide and 10 deep**. The causeway enters at
the south; the loft stairs and landing exit are north. A cart across the center
leaves a two-square passage on either side. Two waist-high stone remnants provide
heavy cover where a narrow attack path remains; they do not make targets invisible.
Give the bow a position behind one wall, not an inaccessible firing platform.
The western bank path remains an exit. Draw these features before a fight starts.

Brin demands **10 coins per traveler** but accepts a credible alternative that
gets the crew away with supplies. The heroes can challenge the quarantine using
the bell chart, talk to individual crew members, pay, bypass the yard, or fight.
A visibly forged notice carries no official seal; inspecting it reveals this
without a roll. Exposure makes Brin willing to leave if the heroes allow escape.

If violence starts, the crew uses its printed attacks and maneuvers. They have no
surprise turn, free opening volley or automatic disarm. Follow the normal initiative
procedure. When Brin falls or two crew members are out of the fight, the others
try to withdraw or surrender. That is their chosen response to danger, not a new
morale roll. Their lethal attacks remain lethal; a surrender must happen before
0 HP if they are to survive without an explicit exception.

The crew does not pursue through the signal room. Allow the heroes to learn that
boundary by watching or talking to them. If the crew escapes, it does not
immediately return fully healed as an unbudgeted second wave.

## 3. The bell loft

Nera has wedged the inner stair door shut. She lowers a rope when the heroes make
contact and can unbar the exterior door once they reach the stairs. No roll is
needed for her to admit obvious rescuers after they speak.

She explains that the sentries stopped when she fled beyond the painted line.
She has not seen Tovin leave the stores. She will accompany a careful rescue but
has no special combat actions. Her broken bell spar explains the irregular signal.
Fixing it or giving her a suitable tool lets her ring the correct signal without
requiring a crafting recipe for an ordinary improvised handle.

## 4. The service hatch

Mud has swollen the hatch against its frame. A safe, unhurried approach with suitable
leverage opens it after a few minutes without a roll. If the bandits are following,
announce that three heroes can reach it, with **one binary group attempt, target 7**,
before their pursuers arrive. Each contributes a Brawn result. Below 7, the hatch
stays closed and the pursuers arrive; do not add damage that was never at stake.

This group procedure is outside combat. If combat has already begun, each hero
must use their own permitted action instead; do not import the group attempt into
the turn order. The heroes may still negotiate, retreat or use another route.

Beyond the hatch, Tovin can crawl out of the alcove without crossing the sentries'
boundary. He brings the keeper log. It contains a sketch of the threshold and the
old instruction to deny passage into the signal room. Reading it requires no
identification spell. It does not grant a command over arbitrary undead.

## 5. The painted threshold

Use [three Skeleton Sentries](../../bestiary/creatures/bone-sentry.md),
**CV 21 total**. Their bodies and swords are visible through the entrance.
Dry clicking begins when someone approaches. A faded red stripe crosses the floor.
One sentry steps forward as a stick or other object passes over the stripe, then
returns to its post when the object is withdrawn. Show this warning before an
unaware character crosses.

Draw an **8-by-8-square chamber**, with a two-square entrance at the south, the
stores corridor outside its western wall, and an old signal mechanism against the
north wall. Broken benches form two patches of difficult terrain. The guardians
remain inside their assigned room. They are not standing shoulder to shoulder in
a one-square entrance that prevents every alternative approach.

The rescue and reopening of the ordinary landing do not require entry. Tovin's
route and Nera's stairs remain outside the boundary. The heroes may secure the
outer door and warn future workers, leaving the guardians in place. Fighting is
an option if the party wants to clear the chamber, not a hidden condition of Mara's
payment. The guardians have no additional reinforcements or powers.

If the party fights here immediately after the toll yard without a safe breath,
consider the accumulated pressure. Do not call the pair of encounters a single
standard fight just because each total is below 40. Both rosters and all expended
resources belong in the session's record.

## Clues that prevent a dead end

| Necessary understanding | Ways to learn it |
|---|---|
| The bell is a rescue request, not proof of plague | Oren's memory; his chart; speaking with Nera. |
| There is a route around the toll yard | The shelter's map; the visible maintenance marker; Oren's account. |
| The sentries guard a boundary | Nera's account; observing the threshold; the keeper log. |
| Tovin can leave without fighting the sentries | The service route; Nera's description of the alcove; Tovin answering a call. |

If the heroes call out from a safe place, let survivors answer when they can hear.
The adventure does not require the players to discover every clue or enter every room.

## If the heroes delay

At dusk, Brin's crew intends to leave with what it has collected. Tell the heroes
about their packed bags and arguments over departure. Unless the heroes intervene,
the crew departs along the causeway; it does not murder the workers off-screen to
force urgency. Nera stops ringing when exhausted and takes shelter in the loft.
The quiet bell then means the signal stopped, not automatic proof that she died.
The sentries maintain their boundary.

These developments change information and opportunities without forcing a prescribed
ending. Change their timing if the opening occurs at another hour, and communicate
the signs before a decision depends on them.

## Rewards and aftermath

Mara pays the **80-coin company reward** when the survivors are out and safe access
is restored. With four heroes, an equal share is 20 each. The rescued workers offer
a **Rope Coil** and **Lever and Wedges**, worth 5 coins each, from their own supplies.
These are physical items with normal Bulk, not new abilities. The party may decline
them or return them after the work. Intact recovered enemy gear uses normal resale
and trade; do not award its sale price as coins as well as handing over the gear.

No reward requires killing the bandits or skeletons. Oren remembers who helped,
and the keeper log may point to another station whose lamp still burns. If you
choose this rescue as a level milestone, award it for resolving the adventure,
not for clearing every creature from the map. Level advancement remains the DA's
decision, not an automatic rule attached to this example.
