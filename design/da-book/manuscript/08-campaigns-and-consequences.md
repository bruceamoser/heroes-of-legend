# Campaigns and Lasting Consequences

The company returns with the missing workers and a story the village will repeat
for years. One hero returns with a scar. Another has spent the last Grit they were
saving for a desperate hour. The road is open again, but the people who opened it
have changed.

A campaign follows both kinds of consequence: what the heroes change in the world
and what their adventures leave with them. Let victories last, let costs matter,
and make room for the players to choose what comes next.

## Award levels for meaningful progress

Use milestones when the heroes resolve an important problem, achieve a shared
goal or reach a significant turning point. A negotiated settlement can qualify
just as well as a battle. Tell the group what kind of progress you intend to
recognize so they do not feel required to seek unnecessary fights.

Help players create level-1 heroes by choosing ancestry and two traits, one discipline each from culture, background and class, one class signature, then spend starting DP on techniques and spells. Starting DP is 10 + Guile + Knowledge + Reason, plus explicit benefits.

The DA awards milestone levels. From level 2 onward, gain **6 + the new level DP**, plus explicit trait benefits. Save points or spend them when the DA judges you are somewhere safe. Add ancestry HP + class HP to maximum HP. Add the same amount to current HP if the hero is above 0 HP. At 0 HP, increase only the maximum. Add **2 maximum SP**, keeping current SP unchanged. Neither a level gain nor a signature change refreshes spent Grit.

Ask each player to record what they learned and the DP they spent. Their class lists what it costs to begin an additional discipline. When they reach levels 3 or 7, point them to the entries they can now learn. Check that earlier entries in the same path or book are already learned.

Your SP reserve is separate from HP. Your level contributes **2 × level SP**: 2 at level 1, 6 at level 3 and 14 at level 7. Add your ancestry’s SP contribution and **twice your positive class casting attribute**. An attribute of 0 or less contributes 0 SP. Add any increases learned through Spellcraft’s Soul Capacity or Mind Capacity paths and other explicit SP benefits. From level 2 onward, gain **2 maximum SP each level**. These increases enlarge capacity without restoring spent SP. Track current SP separately. A safe overnight rest fills the reserve; learned recovery follows its own timing.

## Safe places and downtime

Tell the group whether a place is safe before they commit to a rest.

**Brief Rest.** One uninterrupted safe hour restores **6 HP and 3 SP**, up to
maximum, once per day. First Aid and learned Spellcraft Recovery can add to
that recovery. Apply their own time, supply and use limits. A Dying hero must
be stabilized before recovering from rest.

**Night’s Rest.** Eight safe hours with food, water and adequate shelter
restore full HP and SP. Include six hours of sleep or its equivalent. A
Night’s Rest renews First Aid and uses that refresh overnight, with those
benefits available once in 24 hours. A fight, forced travel or strenuous
work interrupts the rest. Brief waking and routine watch can fit within it.

Track current HP and SP between encounters. First Aid takes ten minutes and
works on its own or alongside a rest. A shorter pause can also give the heroes
time to spend DP in a safe place. Let pressure come from time, enemies and
objectives already in play. Honor the safety you established for the group.

## Keep the survival sequence clear

When a hero falls to 0 HP, they enter Dying, fall prone and lose consciousness.
Start two fresh tallies. At the start of each Dying turn, call for a Fortitude
death roll. Weak adds one failure, Standard leaves both tallies as they are,
and Strong adds one success. Use the result band even on retained triples.

The third success stabilizes the hero at 0 HP. The third failure kills them
unless Grit or an ability intervenes immediately. Each damaging event while
already at 0 HP calls for one immediate death roll. If the hero was stable,
start a fresh Dying episode before rolling.

Anyone can spend a Maneuver to stabilize an adjacent Dying hero. This clears
both tallies and ends Dying. The hero is conscious and prone at 0 HP with three
defense Banes. They can speak and accept aid. They regain actions and voluntary
movement when HP returns, subject to other conditions. Healing above 0 also
clears both tallies and ends Dying and HP-caused unconsciousness.

The player can spend one Grit when their hero falls to 0 HP, at the start of a
Dying turn, immediately after a death roll adds a failure (including the third),
or while stabilized at 0 HP. Spending Grit costs no action or Reaction. Set
current HP to half maximum HP, rounded up, clear both tallies, and end Dying
and unconsciousness caused by HP loss. Other conditions remain. Apply any limit
on HP available for recovery. Heroes gain one Grit at each of levels 1, 3 and 7.
Record grants and expenditures separately. Each point is spent permanently.

Keep turn timing while Dying or immediate hazards remain. Ordinary NPCs die
at 0 HP. Summoned forms disappear.

## Treatment and magical healing

First Aid takes ten uninterrupted minutes, a first-aid supply and a Knowledge
roll: restore 2/4/6 HP. Each recipient has one attempt between safe overnight rests,
shared across all helpers and treatment abilities. Mark it when the attempt
begins, even if interrupted. Treatment ignores Life resistance. Stabilization and
condition-removal actions do not use this allowance.

Learned treatment improves that attempt. Magical healing instead spends the
caster's SP and can be repeated while SP remains; it does not consume first aid.
A potion uses its printed inventory die. Apply Life resistance to magical healing
and potions. Restore at least 1 HP if any is missing, up to maximum HP.

A healer with no separately printed SP reserve has 6 SP. Summoned healers spend
their controller's SP and cannot receive healing. A safe overnight rest restores
all SP. A hero with [Spellcraft Recovery](../../disciplines/spellcraft.md#recovery)
can add its printed SP to a Brief Rest or recover it during a separate pause.
Spend one uninterrupted hour in safety resting, meditating, or enjoying quiet leisure. Use the amount restored by the hero’s learned Recovery ability, up to maximum SP. The hero can take this recovery pause once between safe overnight rests. Perfect Repose shortens it to 30 minutes. Learning an improvement after using the pause leaves that use spent.

## Scars and a hero's death

A survived incident that brought a hero to 0 leaves one descriptive scar. Several
falls during that incident do not require several rolls or injury records. Ask the
player what they wish to remember. Scars carry no penalties, Boons or treatment
requirements. Do not turn a cosmetic description into a lost limb or compelled
behavior. [Scars and Survival](../../rules/wounds.md) offers prompts.

If a hero dies, give their player a moment for Last Words, a final gesture or silence. Abilities
that prevent death resolve at their stated triggers. Returning a dead hero
to life requires a resurrection ritual.

Let the company decide what comes next: a funeral, a journey home, a promise or
an urgent escape. Discuss a replacement character promptly so the player can
return to participation. Agree on entry circumstances and resources openly.

## Capture, retreat and retirement

Attacks are lethal by default, and ordinary opponents die at 0 HP. When a player
declares a nonlethal option before rolling, use the outcome printed in their ability.
For example, a nonlethal Pinning Strike that would drop a living target to 0 HP
leaves it at 1 HP and unconscious for 1 minute or until it takes damage again.

An adjacent character with a free hand can spend a Maneuver to apply suitable
manacles to a surrendered or helpless creature, or one at Grappled 3 or higher.
The manacles stay locked if the grapplers release their holds. Use the limbs bound
to judge what the captive can still do. They can be freed with the key, or through
an escape objective if their means make escape possible.

When escape is possible, describe it. Withdrawing may cost equipment, position or
an objective, but those consequences should follow the scene. Do not promise safety
and then invent pursuit that the opposition had no way to organize.

A player may choose retirement before death. Their hero can become a local ally,
a mentor or someone the next company hopes to impress. Preserve that person's
achievements without turning them into a free source of unlimited abilities or
supplies. The campaign's continuity comes from its relationships and consequences,
not from requiring the same four people to survive every chapter.
