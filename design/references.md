Page numbers below are the library’s indexed PDF pages, which can differ from
printed folios. These are design references; no foreign ability text was copied.

MCDM also explicitly describes Draw Steel through its heroic, tactical and cinematic
focus in its [publisher introduction](https://www.mcdmproductions.com/). The application
here is our design judgment, not a claim that HOL uses Draw Steel’s rules unchanged.

The [D&D basic play reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/playing-the-game)
describes a play rhythm built around description, player intention and resolution.
HOL keeps that invitation to act while using its own attributes, outcomes and costs.

## Concrete changes after research

- Team movement and displacement create rescues and coordinated openings.
- Utility magic supplies a bridge, a voice, a scout, a light or an unusual route.
- Crafting includes salvage and useful field work rather than only a downtime ledger.
- Flavor introduces what the character does; it does not secretly change damage type.
- Summons have different jobs: moving enemies, protecting an ally, scouting or striking
  with lightning. Their command economy remains HOL’s agreed structure.

These references do not replace HOL’s constraints: no spell slots, no skill bonus
ladder, no attribute growth race, no imported action economy, and no extra resource
pool. Familiar fantasy situations are translated into HOL, with original wording.

## Reading boundary

This was targeted reading, not an exhaustive review of the library. The actual
passages above support the stated design lessons. Broader claims about the three
systems’ comparative balance are not made here. Later authoring can use the library
for specific missing fantasies instead of adapting entire catalogs wholesale.
