# Coins and the ordinary market

**First economy proposal — issue #1166.** All prices are whole **coins**, with no
metal denominations. Prices establish a usable town market, not a historical
currency conversion. These values and new supply policies await Bruce’s approval.

## What a coin buys

A simple meal costs 1 coin. A plain bed costs 2 coins per night, meals separate.
Ordinary local labor pays about 3 coins per day; skilled work about 6. These are
anchors, not a requirement to simulate wages. Dangerous adventuring earns more
because it risks wounds, scarce Grit and death, not because food becomes expensive
when a character gains levels.

Equipment grants still happen first. Coins buy objects and supplies, never DP,
discipline ranks, signature access or training. Owning armor does not grant its
training card. A purchased weapon’s appearance supplies no damage modifier.
Prices for functionally equivalent ordinary melee weapons are equal; a jeweled
hilt may be valuable decoration but does not hit harder.

A worn plain outfit is the explicitly proposed exception; an extra outfit is Bulk 1.

## Supplies and restocking

The printed price buys one full supply at its listed die, including an ordinary
holder. All three healing bottles currently use d4; their potency, not supply die,
differs. This pass preserves those existing values rather than quietly increasing
potion longevity. A die checks after use and on 1 steps down; the last use works.

A d4 averages 4 uses before empty; d6 averages 10 total uses through its d4 stage;
d8 averages 18. These are expectations, not guaranteed charges. The inventory die
cannot be converted into an exact number of arrows or spoonfuls.

**Proposed shop policy:** a shop can refill a supply to its printed starting die
for its full listed supply price. Or buy a separate full supply at that price,
which costs another Bulk. No discounted partial refill, die combining, decanting
into fresh full-die bottles or cash credit for unknown leftovers. This keeps the
market simple without changing crafting’s existing step-increase procedure.
Fresh safe water can refill its flask freely; a town refill costs 1 coin if charged.

A service fee buys the provider’s time, not an automatic Strong outcome. Daily
wound-treatment limits still apply across all healers. Inn lodging does not refill
Grit or erase scars. Transport quotes grant no mount statistics or summon eligibility.
Hired people have agency; wages do not buy disposable combatants or unlimited
ability access. Extended hazardous work needs a specific agreement with the DA.

## Availability, selling and crafting

These prices assume a supplied town. Ordinary travel goods are common. Specialist
tools, potions and unusual supplies need an appropriate seller. Special equipment
prices are valuation benchmarks: the DA decides whether an item is actually on
sale. No automatic stock refresh after a rest or new scene.

For an unusual shortage or remote seller, the DA may quote double ordinary prices;
otherwise use the printed value rather than maintaining regional multipliers.
Record a concrete offer before buying. Exact scarcity and merchant stock remain
adventure decisions, not a universal cap on player purchases.

**Agreed sale/trade rule:** intact, useful gear sells for half list price,
rounded down, or trades for its full listed value with a willing trader. A 12-coin weapon sells for 6 coins or trades for 12 coins of goods. Pay any shortfall in coins; unused trade value is not cash change. A trade cannot be redeemed for coins, vouchers or other cash equivalents at full value. Card-granted items use the same rule, and selling
never renews their grant. Consumables and materials with uncertain remaining supply
have no standard resale offer. Goods freshly crafted or acquired at full supply
may attract half-price offers, but the buyer must actually need that quantity.
Special items still require an available willing trader; apply the same half-sale/full-trade rule to the agreed item valuation. Treasure such as gems has its stated
trade value, without a second automatic half-price reduction.

All material bundles must name their actual contents. Ordinary d6 ingredients
cost 12 coins; a d6 prepared healing-ingredient bundle costs 80. Other rare inputs
need a quote. Materials must meet the recipe: a common bundle cannot become life
salts by relabeling it. Recipes are still found, derived or granted through cards,
not automatically available for coin purchase.

Crafting remains useful. Gathering ingredients, knowing recipes and spending time
can save coins. This is not a closed or proven macroeconomy: foraging success,
recipe yields, local demand and available downtime determine profit. A merchant’s
willing purchase is not an infinite exchange machine. Large production should be
reviewed before assigning guaranteed commercial income.

## Shopping bundles and starting range

Bundles are convenience lists at the exact sum of their parts, with no discount or
Bulk exemption. Each person still chooses what to carry.

- **Road essentials: 24 coins, 5 Bulk.** Pack 3 + sleeping roll 4 + filled water
  flask 3 + d8 food 9 + d6 lighting 5. One plain worn outfit is proposed free.
- **Self-contained explorer: 31 coins, 7 Bulk.** Road essentials + rope 5 and small
  necessities 2. At Brawn −1, this alone fills capacity 7.
- **Road essentials plus lesser healing: 44 coins, 6 Bulk.** Add a 20-coin d4 bottle.
- **Explorer plus lesser healing: 51 coins, 8 Bulk.** Useful, but too much for a
  Brawn −1 character without sharing a load or accepting a Speed penalty.

A spell light can replace a purchased lighting supply if the group accepts its
limitations. Sharing rope, light and camp tools is a choice, not a free group
inventory. Card-granted items remain part of carried Bulk even though they cost
no coins at creation.

**Agreed starting target: 50–70 coins after card grants.** At 50, a
character can buy road essentials, a lesser potion and retain 6 coins; at 70 that
same choice leaves 26 for specialist purchases or reserves. The fully equipped
explorer with a potion costs 51, so the low end requires a small tradeoff. This
is a proposed range, not an assigned character balance or an approved formula.

The [proposed starting allocation](starting-coins.md) supplies base + ancestry + culture + background + class values, all within that target.
