# Learning and using disciplines

A discipline offers four paths of practice. Spend 1 DP to begin studying it, then choose a path and learn its first ability. Within each path, learn the first ability, its two improvements and then its final ability, in that order. The four steps cost 1, 2, 2 and 3 DP. Each entry is learned once. Improvements remain active together and affect only the named ability. The final ability is separate unless it explicitly uses the earlier technique.

You may learn disciplines and their first three steps during level-0 or level-1 development. The final ability requires character level 3. I names the first ability. II and III mark its improvements. Learning one path never requires learning another path in that discipline. Every hero can make basic attacks, speak, climb, search and help someone. Discipline paths develop those everyday capabilities into practiced techniques.

When an improvement replaces a number, use the new total rather than adding both totals. For example, Driving Blow II pushes 2 squares instead of 1; Driving Blow III adds Dazed 1. On Strong, both the 2-square push and Dazed apply. There is no choice between them. Only explicit choices such as a destination or target remain.

## Common action rules

Damage and progress written as three numbers mean Weak / Standard / Strong. Roll 3d6 plus the stated attribute, applying normal Boons and Banes: Weak is 8 or less, Standard 9–14, Strong 15 or more. For attacks against your hero, you roll the printed defense. For exchanges between NPCs, a player or the DA rolls using the NPC procedure.

Every attack hits. Remove immune damage types, then apply weakness, matching resistance and other reductions. Keep the final minimum of 1 if any positive damage component survived immunity. Reducing a condition or forced movement to 0 is permitted: those are not damage. Attack damage is instant; conditions follow their normal timing and decay unless an ability states a duration. Stability and any explicit forced-movement defenses still apply.

An ability costs its printed action every time it is used. A Reaction needs its stated trigger and an available Reaction; no entry here grants additional Reactions. An improvement costs no separate action unless stated. Passive training works while its requirements hold. A time such as 10 minutes is the uninterrupted work required for each roll; it is not a combat Main Action. Normal condition restrictions and action conversions still apply.

Targets must be reachable as the ability describes. Movement requires an unoccupied legal destination and a feasible route; it stops at barriers. When forced movement meets a creature or barrier, use the collision rules in Playing the Game. Other movement uses the usual movement-reaction rules unless the entry explicitly avoids them. An ability with a choice of up to several targets can affect each target only once per use.

Gather the equipment your chosen technique uses. An entry that provides equipment describes what you receive. Ordinary recipes and ingredients retain their existing inventory and price rules. Smoke ingredients use one ordinary recipe ingredient batch per flask, with price and availability set before preparation; finished flasks have Bulk 1 each. Prepared products persist until used or spoiled. Craft covers ordinary construction and preparation; use the appropriate magical working to create enchanted items.

# Objectives and progress

Use an objective when success takes more than one useful contribution: repairing a gate, following a trail, translating a passage or convincing a reluctant listener. Ordinary actions remain available without a learned ability. Learned abilities change the time, progress, reach or effects of those actions.

## Set the objective before rolling

The Dungeon Architect states the concrete result, what evidence or resources are needed, and how much progress completes it. Use 3 progress for a modest task, 6 for a substantial task, 9 for a demanding task and 12 for an exceptional task. These are reference values; the DA chooses the objective level appropriate to the situation. Impossible tasks do not become possible through accumulated rolls.

The DA also states the work interval, deadline and known consequences. A printed ability supplies its own interval and progress results. An ordinary feasible contribution adds 1/2/3 progress with the attribute appropriate to the approach. Progress is shared only when contributions genuinely serve the same objective. Another person may Aid or make their own contribution during an interval, not both. Craft projects remain individual.

At the target, resolve the stated result. Extra progress does not spill into unrelated objectives. Weak still makes progress; it is not a failure to act. Natural retained 111 adds a complication, and 666 adds a benefit, as in the core resolution rules. No routine extra penalty is attached merely to a Weak result. The DA may track a separate deadline or danger established before the attempt.

## Social encounters

State the concession sought and who can grant it. The DA determines whether persuasion, evidence, deception, a bargain or pressure can work. Set one objective for listeners who share a motive; use separate objectives for listeners with different interests. State known boundaries and deal-breakers before the roll when the characters could recognize them.

An exchange is one meaningful statement or response, normally about a minute outside combat. In combat, an exchange costs a Main Action. Listeners must be able to perceive and understand the approach. A new roll needs a new relevant fact, offer, demonstration or changed circumstance. Repeating the same argument earns no progress. Different speakers cannot obtain extra rolls by repeating it.

Completing an attainable objective earns the agreed concession, not control over the listener. It cannot override a player's decisions, invent consent, erase an enemy's motives or force an impossible bargain. Changed circumstances may require a new objective; the DA explains why existing progress no longer answers the problem. Unsupported claims may be inapplicable, but a permitted Weak roll still advances the objective.

Example: a keeper needs assurance before opening a gate. The DA sets 6 progress to gain entry, requiring a credible reason and a guarantee against trouble. A Standard Make the Case II contributes 3. A companion presents a relevant letter and rolls Standard on an ordinary Reason contribution for 2. The first speaker adds a new offer and rolls Weak for 1: the gate opens under the agreed terms.

## Medicine and recovery

With suitable supplies, anyone can give first aid. Spend 10 minutes and roll Knowledge to restore 2/4/6 HP. Each recipient can receive one first-aid treatment between safe overnight rests. The recipient can use that attempt for ordinary first aid or a learned treatment such as Field Dressing. All healers share the same allowance.

Condition care treats specific conditions. Apply the treatment’s listed condition reductions and any HP recovery it provides.

A safe overnight rest takes 8 hours, including 6 hours of sleep and 2 quiet hours, and restores HP and SP once per 24 hours. Shelter alone does not guarantee safety. Track treatment through HP and the recipient’s first-aid allowance. Scars remain part of the hero’s story. Use the Stabilize Maneuver to help an adjacent Dying hero. The hero stays at 0 HP and clears both death roll tallies. Further damage at 0 causes an immediate death roll.
