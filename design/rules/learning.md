# Learning and using disciplines

Your culture, background and class each give you a discipline to study. To begin another, spend the 1, 2 or 3 DP listed for your class. Choose a path in that discipline and learn its entries in order, paying the listed DP. You can study several paths. To learn an entry, meet its level requirement and learn the earlier entries in that path.

When you learn an improvement, apply it along with the benefits you already learned for that ability. If it gives a new value for damage, range or another number, use that value in place of the old one. Advanced entries become available at level 3 and masteries at level 7. Read each entry to see whether it teaches a new ability or improves one you already know.

To study a spellbook, learn its sections in order and add the spells marked for each section. See [Magic and spellbooks](magic.md) for the section costs and levels. In Spellcraft, study Soul Capacity or Mind Capacity to hold more SP, Recovery to restore spent SP, or Channeling to transfer it.

Every hero can make basic attacks, speak, climb, search and help. Learned abilities give you more ways to act. Read their requirements and gather any gear you need.

## Common action rules

Damage and progress written as three numbers mean Weak / Standard / Strong. Roll 3d6 plus the stated attribute, applying normal Boons and Banes: Weak is 8 or less, Standard 9–14, Strong 15 or more. For attacks against your hero, you roll the printed defense. For exchanges between NPCs, a player or the DA rolls using the NPC procedure.

Every attack hits. Remove immune damage types, then apply weakness, matching resistance and other reductions. Keep the final minimum of 1 if any positive damage component survived immunity. Reducing a condition or forced movement to 0 is permitted: those are not damage. Attack damage is instant; conditions follow their normal timing and decay unless an ability states a duration. Stability and any explicit forced-movement defenses still apply.

An ability costs its printed action every time it is used. Spend an available Reaction when its stated trigger occurs. Outside combat, follow the ability’s limit per exchange, task or event. An improvement costs no separate action unless stated. Passive training works while its requirements hold. A time such as 10 minutes is the uninterrupted work required for each roll; it is not a combat Main Action. Normal condition restrictions and action conversions still apply.

Targets must be reachable as the ability describes. Movement requires an unoccupied legal destination and a feasible route; it stops at barriers. When forced movement meets a creature or barrier, use the collision rules in Playing the Game. Other movement uses the usual movement-reaction rules unless the entry explicitly avoids them. An ability with a choice of up to several targets can affect each target only once per use.

Gather the equipment your chosen technique uses. An entry that provides equipment describes what you receive. Use the inventory and price rules for recipes and ingredients. Smoke ingredients use one ordinary recipe ingredient batch per flask, with price and availability set before preparation; finished flasks have Bulk 1 each. Prepared products persist until used or spoiled. Craft covers ordinary construction and preparation; use the appropriate magical working to create enchanted items.

For tasks that take several contributions, use Objectives and progress in the play procedures. For first aid, treatment and rest, see the recovery chapter.
