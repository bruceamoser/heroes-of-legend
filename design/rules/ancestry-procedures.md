# Reach, defense and recovery

Ancestry traits use the ordinary action, targeting and recovery procedures together with their printed grants.

## Touch and object interactions

Ordinary physical reach extends to an adjacent square. A touch ability is one whose rules or narrative require contact with its target. An ability’s printed adjacency requirement describes distance; its description establishes whether it uses physical contact.

An explicit reach grant gives its printed distance for the interactions it names. Long Reach gives Tallfolk a range of 2 squares for object interactions and non-attack touch abilities. Use the ordinary action cost each time: picking up an object costs a Maneuver, and a touch ability spends its printed action and costs. Attack range follows the attack’s printed rules.

For example, a Tallfolk with Long Reach can pick up a key 2 squares away with a Maneuver, or use a healing touch ability on an ally 2 squares away. Each use follows its ordinary action cost.

## Defense Boons when the attacker rolls

When the defender rolls, apply defense Boons and Banes to that defense roll. When a player-controlled attacker makes the roll instead, each applicable defense Boon becomes 1 Bane to the attack, and each applicable defense Bane becomes 1 Boon to the attack. Combine those with the attacker’s own Boons and Banes normally.

For example, Small Target gives a gnome 1 Boon to defense rolls. Against an attack rolled by another player, that benefit instead supplies 1 Bane to the attacker’s roll.

## Oath-Bound recovery

Oath-Bound count as living creatures for healing and use the ordinary hero rules for HP, wounds, rest and recovery.

## Paying HP costs

When an ability asks you to spend HP, pay its printed amount. This cost bypasses resistance and must leave you with at least 1 HP.

## HP unavailable for recovery

When an ability makes spent HP unavailable for recovery until a stated time, record that HP and its release time. During that period, healing and a safe breath can restore HP up to maximum HP minus the unavailable amount. Record this separately from wounds and maximum HP.

For example, an Oath-Bound with maximum HP 20 uses The Debt Comes Due and spends 1 HP. Until the next dawn, healing or a safe breath can restore them to 19 HP. At dawn, that HP becomes available for ordinary recovery again.

A reroll replaces the original roll for the same action or event. Use the original attribute and applicable Boons and Banes, roll again, and resolve the new result, including its retained natural dice.
