# Perception, interruption and movement

## Player rolls — agreed

When a player looks for something, the DA calls for **Reason**, with Boons/Banes
for circumstances. When someone might perceive a hidden player character, that
player rolls **Agility** to remain Hidden. Do not add an opposed observer roll.
Ordinary obvious information requires no roll. Hidden remains universal; actual
concealment and a plausible approach matter.

**Procedure proposals to review:** active searching or attempting to hide during
combat costs a Main Action unless a card states otherwise. A hidden creature that
attacks or openly reveals itself loses Hidden. No roll conceals someone in plain
view with no plausible concealment. These action costs and the exact Weak/Standard/
Strong information or concealment outcomes remain to be approved; the attribute
choice alone does not settle what a Weak stealth result means.

## Opportunities — agreed

Starting a provoking action triggers the opportunity attack **before** that action
resolves. Resolve Basic Melee using the attacker’s normal player-roll procedure;
no specialty attack. Then the original action continues if its actor is still
capable; otherwise it stops. Damage alone does not automatically cancel the action.
Forced movement never provokes.

Leaving melee reach voluntarily, casting a spell beside an enemy, standing,
stowing and retrieving items are the intended triggers established in discussion.
Drawing as part of an attack stays part of that attack. **Timing proposal:** commit
the action when declared; check targets and capability again after interruption;
pay an additional printed HP/item cost only when continuing into its resolution.
If interrupted permanently, the committed action is spent but an unspent potion or
HP cost is not consumed. This cost-order detail needs approval; it is not inferred
from “continues if capable.”

## Player-controlled creatures — agreed

Creature cards must print attack and defense attributes. The controlling player
rolls those printed creature attributes, not their character’s attribute. When an
NPC attacks a player-controlled summon, its controller rolls the summon’s defense.
When the summon attacks, its controller makes the attack roll. Use the ordinary
single-roll attack/defense outcome mapping. A player-controlled attacker does not
also cause a second player defense roll.

A summon disappears when its controller becomes unconscious or dies. An
uncommanded summon does nothing; existing effects and conditions still resolve.
NPC-versus-NPC conflicts with no player controller still need a DA procedure;
this agreement does not assign an imaginary controller to every creature.

## Stability and ordinary movement

**Agreed:** every creature has Stability, normally 0; larger creatures generally
have more. It resists forced movement. **Application proposal:** subtract Stability
once from each push/pull distance before terrain costs, minimum 0. Use the highest
applicable movement-reduction value rather than adding Stability to another such
bonus. Reduce the initial target’s displacement before forming a collision group;
secondary collision members do not repeatedly subtract Stability. That last group
interaction requires testing and approval alongside multi-square creature geometry.

**Agreed:** falling deals **3 HP per 2 squares fallen**. **Rounding/damage proposal:**
use 3 × floor(distance / 2), physical damage with ordinary physical resistance;
falls under 2 squares deal no automatic damage. Landing geometry, Prone and
exceptional surfaces remain DA rulings until specified. “HP” did not itself settle
whether this bypasses resistance, so that interpretation is explicitly proposed.

**Agreed:** climbing and swimming use difficult-terrain movement cost, normally
2 per square, unless an ability changes it. Impossible surfaces and impassable
currents do not become traversable merely by spending movement. Roll only when
uncertainty has consequences. Cards can improve the ordinary activity.

**Jumping — approved:** broad jump up to max(1, 2 + Agility) squares; high jump up to
max(0, 1 + floor(Agility / 2)) squares. Each square traveled spends movement; a
broad jump needs 2 squares of approach, otherwise halve its maximum, rounded down
(minimum 1). A high jump needs no approach. Land in a legal supported free square;
no extra movement or passage through barriers. Negative Agility can mean no full
square of high-jump clearance, not inability to hop over a small obstacle. Obstacles
below a square and risky landings are adjudicated normally. No roll for a clear,
safe jump within these limits; risky execution can call for Agility. These values are approved; specialist movement cards retain their explicit exceptions.

## Consequence vocabulary — guidance proposal

A **consequence** is what is at stake: harm, lost time, exposure, lost supplies,
a worsened position or an opportunity that closes. A **complication** introduces
an additional problem while retaining whatever progress the roll achieved. A
**benefit** adds a useful advantage. State the stakes before rolling. Weak is
progress, not automatic success; Strong is success with the printed result. Natural
111 adds a complication to Weak; 666 adds a benefit to Strong.

Use **1 Bane** for a meaningful obstacle, **2** for a severe obstacle, **3** for an
exceptional obstacle that remains possible. Use Boons symmetrically for real help
or advantageous circumstances. These are DA guidance bands, not target numbers.
Cancel Boons/Banes and apply the existing net cap of 3. Do not roll impossible
attempts or apply the same obstacle twice under different names. Group progress
and binary/cumulative task targets keep their existing rules.

## Magic boundaries — agreed

Countermagic uses defensive interference and disruption; a separate generic spell
cancellation system is not required. Healing used against a healing-harmed unwilling
creature resolves as an attack spell, like Flame Dart: use the spell’s printed
attribute and result as Life damage, normal attack targeting and resistance.
Keep the healing spell’s action, range, free hand and upfront life cost. Beneficial
recovery riders do not help that target. Eligible creature cards must print the
appropriate defense attribute; no extra opposed save is added. Ordinary willing
healing retains its existing procedure.
