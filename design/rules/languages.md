# Languages

A familiar greeting can open a door that gold cannot. An inscription can preserve
an enemy's warning long after the enemy is dust. Languages tell you whom your hero
can understand and which voices shaped their life. They do not determine ancestry,
allegiance or character.

**Status:** New language defaults proposed for Bruce’s review, issue #1265. Existing card benefits remain unchanged.

## Choose your languages

Begin with **Common and one other ordinary language** learned in the community
that raised you. Common is the campaign's shared trade language; the DA may give
it a local name. Choose your other language with the DA from the list below or
the campaign's equivalent. Any ancestry may know any language. Culture suggests
where you learned it, rather than imposing a compulsory choice.

Record the language's actual name, not just “regional” or “community language.”
If Common was your only home language, choose another ordinary language learned
through neighbors, work or travel. This starting choice is separate from your
culture's discipline and ability pair and costs no DP.

Apply explicit extra grants afterward. **A Foot in Two Worlds** grants two additional
ordinary spoken languages. Choose two distinct languages you do not already know;
it does not grant another culture's abilities. A signed language is an ordinary
language when the campaign uses it, but a grant restricted to *spoken* languages
does not include it.

Unless a grant says otherwise, knowing a language includes speaking, understanding,
and reading and writing its usual script, if it has one. A grant that specifically
says *spoken*, *understands* or *cannot speak* keeps that restriction. Record such
limits beside the language. Speaking a language does not require a separate skill
rating, and attributes grant no automatic extra languages.

## Ordinary languages

These are starting options, not a required history of the world. Their names
describe familiar traditions; anyone raised among their speakers may learn them.
Each row is one language unless it explicitly asks you to name a particular one.

| Language | Where you might learn or encounter it |
|---|---|
| Common | Markets, mixed settlements, roads and adventuring companies. |
| Regional language | Choose one named local language: a river kingdom, island community or frontier valley. Each is a separate choice. |
| Dwarven | Deep halls, quarry towns and craft communities; records of lineage, work and agreements. |
| Elven | Woodland settlements and long-lived artistic traditions; songs, histories and correspondence. |
| Halfling | Hearth communities, traveling families and river villages; stories and everyday business. |
| Goblin | Goblin communities and their neighbors; salvage yards, markets and warrens. |
| Orc | Orc communities and their neighbors; border settlements, expeditions and oral histories. |
| Giant | Giant communities and peoples who trade with them; landmarks, stone records and remembered journeys. |
| Kobold | Kobold communities and their neighbors; tunnel works, workshops and trading posts. |
| Harbor Cant | Multilingual ports and ship crews. A full local language, not universal knowledge of every sailor's slang. |
| Way Sign | A complete signed language shared by participating communities. Communication requires sight and usable signing gestures. |

## Uncommon and ancient languages

These languages are not included in an unrestricted *ordinary language* grant.
Choose one only when a grant explicitly allows it or the DA makes it an ordinary
option for the campaign before character creation. Their availability reflects
the setting, not a higher numerical bonus.

| Language | Where it becomes useful |
|---|---|
| Draconic | Speaking with dragons and their learned allies; reading draconic inscriptions. Kobold is a separate language. |
| Fey | Negotiating with fey courts and woodland powers; understanding their stories and spoken bargains. |
| Infernal | Speaking with fiends and interpreting their correspondence or contracts. Knowing it does not bind or summon anything. |
| Celestial | Conversations with celestial beings and some sacred traditions. It grants no divine authority. |
| Elemental | Speech used by elemental beings capable of language. It does not let you command fire, weather or an unintelligent elemental. |
| Elder Tongue | Choose one named lost or ancient scholarly language. Different vanished civilizations need not share it. |

A sacred text may be written in Common, Dwarven or any other language. “Sacred,”
“scholarly” and “courtly” describe use, not automatic additional languages.
Beasts' calls, a spirit's images and a construct's signal lights are forms of
communication, not languages you gain by choosing a row in these tables.

## Use languages at the table

When you share a language and can hear, see or read it as required, ordinary
communication needs no roll. State what you say or ask. A conversation can still
involve uncertainty about persuasion, deception or evidence; use the normal
attribute or the ability you declared. Knowing the words does not make the listener
agree, reveal secrets or recognize your authority.

A language barrier is not simply extra Banes on a roll. Without a shared means of
communication, you cannot deliver a precise argument merely by rolling well.
Pointing, drawing or demonstrating can convey something simple: “danger there,”
“follow me,” or an offered trade. Agree on what is actually understood. An interpreter,
a translation, or an applicable ability can make a more complicated exchange possible.
A card that explicitly requires a shared language still requires one; an interpreter
does not silently change its target requirement.

Reading a clear message in a language you can read needs no roll. Damaged text,
an unfamiliar technical subject or a cipher may present a separate problem.
Sharing an alphabet does not grant another language, and a cipher is not an extra
language choice. Ask the DA what evidence or expertise could make progress possible.

**Translate the Fragment** supplies a way to interpret unfamiliar written text.
Use its printed time, progress and outcomes. Completing a translation does not
teach you the whole language. A copied voice or disguise likewise supplies no
vocabulary or memories unless its ability explicitly says so.

## Keep learning grounded

Record new languages when an explicit trait, ability or agreed campaign grant
provides them. Time with a teacher, a community or useful texts explains that
learning; it does not create an unstated free advancement or a new universal DP
price. Agree on the route before spending time or DP. Translation and ordinary
practice remain useful even when they do not grant permanent fluency.

> **At the gate.** An elf raised in a dwarven mining town chooses Common and
> Dwarven. She reads the quarry's warning without a roll. Its damaged diagram
> still needs investigation. Her human companion's extra spoken Goblin lets him
> question a salvager, but does not let him read the salvager's written ledger.
