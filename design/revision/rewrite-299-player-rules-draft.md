## Developing your techniques

Each nonmagical discipline offers four paths. Its northern path develops a Main Action, its western path a Maneuver, and its eastern path a Reaction. The southern path explores another distinctive part of the discipline. Reactions can answer events in conversation, investigation and practical work as well as combat. Each entry tells you when to use it.

Begin with a path's first ability. Its next two entries improve that ability, adding the benefits they describe. At level 3, you can learn the path's major development, then two improvements to it. At level 7, its mastery becomes available. Learn each path in order and record the improvements together with the ability they affect.

On the atlas, circles mark beginning studies, triangles mark level-3 studies, and diamonds mark level-7 masteries. A solid gold symbol marks something you have learned. A gold border around a green symbol marks something you can learn now. Select any symbol to read its full effects and requirements.

Magical disciplines gather themed spellbooks. Learn a book's sections in order to add its spells to your repertoire. Each spell gives its action, SP cost, range, duration and effects. The book's theme brings those spells together; its spells may use different actions. Spellcraft describes its capacity and recovery training separately from its Channeling spellbook.

## Reactions beyond a fight

A Reaction answers a particular event. Read its trigger before using it: a scholar might recognize a misleading claim as it is made, while a craftsperson might catch a failing mechanism before its damage spreads. The entry gives the response's timing and how often it can be used during that activity. Resolving one conversation or work attempt does not require counting combat rounds.

## Lasting and sustained spells

A spell lasts for the time printed in its description. Some effects continue in place and let you spend an action to direct them. A beam of light, for example, can remain where you placed it while you act elsewhere; its spell tells you which action moves it.

Casting a spell marked Sustained maintains it until the end of your next turn. On each turn after casting, spend a Maneuver to keep it active. That Maneuver maintains it through the end of your next turn and costs 0 SP. A Main Action may be converted to the required Maneuver. You may sustain one spell at a time. Starting another sustained spell ends the first. Missing the upkeep, becoming unconscious or dying ends your sustain.

## Breaking a magical hold

A spell that holds a creature tells you how it can struggle free and names the attributes used. The attribute for escaping can differ from the attribute used to cast the spell.

On its turn, a conscious target may spend a Main Action to attempt the printed escape, even while the hold restricts its other actions. When an enemy struggles against your spell, roll the attribute that spell names for maintaining your hold. On Weak, the enemy breaks free. This response uses no action of yours.

When your hero struggles against an enemy's spell, roll the attribute that spell names for escape. On Strong, your hero breaks free. After escaping, you can use any movement, Maneuver or Reaction you still have available. Each escape releases that creature; other affected creatures remain subject to the spell.

A spell's escape instructions apply to its magical hold. Physical restraints, including Grappled 3, follow their own release rules.
