# Ancestry characteristics: first design pass for #299 / #301

**Status: proposed, November 15, 2026.** This document changes no live rules or catalog. Numerical changes and replacement effects below require the integrated rewrite review. The current published book and Workshop remain the playtest baseline until that review. This is an exhaustive proposal for fifty existing trait IDs, not fifty additional choices.

Tracking: [rewrite #299](https://github.com/bruceamoser/heroes-of-legend-foundry/issues/299), [traits/signatures #301](https://github.com/bruceamoser/heroes-of-legend-foundry/issues/301). Evidence: [the fifty-trait Workshop audit](https://github.com/bruceamoser/heroes-of-legend-foundry/blob/main/Build-Offs/ancestry-trait-review.md), current [ancestry procedures](../rules/ancestry-procedures.md), [vision](../rules/vision.md), and individual source files linked below. The earlier audit describes compatibility before #299; its old branch/combined-ability summary is not the new progression proposal.

## Design decision

A trait describes something about this particular hero that makes its rule true: luminous eyes, a patient temperament, an uncanny memory, a body animated by another will. The characteristic and mechanic belong in the same sentences. An illustration or adjective alone does not fulfill that requirement. Characters still choose two traits from five; personality traits describe a chosen inclination, not compulsory behavior for an entire people.

Use a narrow, repeatable Boon, a small permanent benefit, a special permission, or an occasional stronger benefit. Avoid unconditional attribute Boons, universal defense improvements, new combat buttons, and hidden free equipment. These are ancestry characteristics, not substitutes for a discipline's learned technique or a class signature.

The packages below deliberately explore natural tradeoffs rather than mostly retaining existing bonuses beneath new adjectives. Many are optional ways of using a characteristic: methodical hands can work slowly for precision, an advocate can expose their allegiance, or a collector can carry more at the expense of quick retrieval. The drawback is real when that benefit is used; ordinary play remains available without it. A drawback belongs where the characteristic naturally produces it. Glowing eyes can betray a hiding place; attention to vibration can be overwhelmed by a moving floor. A quick stride does not need a fabricated penalty to diplomacy. The matrix names actual side effects separately from ordinary scope and explains when no additional drawback is warranted. No ancestry receives a setting-wide prejudice penalty or mandatory personality flaw.

**Common timing proposal:** replace ancestry trait “once per day” with “once between safe overnight rests,” using the existing once-per-24-hours recovery limit. This consolidates existing limited-use traits into the sheet's recovery controls. The Debt Comes Due retains its specific dawn release for unavailable HP; a dawn does not refresh its use. This is a visible procedural change, not an editorial synonym.

## Preserve the Workshop ancestry resource allocation

The book has older coin values and does not yet express the approved SP allocation consistently. Integrate the existing Workshop values, without using this trait review to rebalance those numbers:

| Ancestry | HP contribution | Starting SP | Starting coins | Base Speed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Human | 2 | 1 | 1 | 6 |
| High Elf | 2 | 2 | 1 | 6 |
| Wood Elf | 2 | 1 | 2 | 6 |
| Hill Dwarf | 3 | 0 | 2 | 5 |
| Deep Dwarf | 2 | 1 | 2 | 5 |
| Halfling | 1 | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| Tallfolk | 3 | 0 | 1 | 6 |
| Gnome | 1 | 3 | 0 | 6 |
| Goblin | 1 | 1 | 1 | 6 |
| Oath-Bound | 2 | 2 | 0 | 6 |

HP contribution repeats with level under the approved formula; starting SP and coins do not. Trait fixed HP remains a separate one-time maximum-HP benefit. Trait Boons never change attribute scores, creation DP, or class SP. Culture, background and class supply the three starting disciplines; ancestry supplies its characteristics. Human borrowing retains the Human row, including Size, and grants the entire borrowed trait, including its physical manifestation and applicable side effects.

## Fifty-trait matrix

“Preserve” keeps the existing substantive effect while supplying characteristic-led wording and the shared recovery convention above. “Modify” adjusts a limit, benefit or procedure without changing the trait's central fantasy. “Replace” substitutes a materially different effect under the stable ID. Text in the benefit column is a candidate for the actual description, not a second detachable benefit list. Normal tools, action costs, range, resistance and Boon/Bane rules continue to apply.

## Power, borrowing and interaction review

1. **Human borrowing makes every trait a cross-ancestry option.** Do not explain a weak trait by ancestry HP alone: a Human may select the better standalone option. Keep the language bonus but borrow the complete rule, including glowing eyes, vibration limits or unpaid HP. Preserve the chosen trait ID in the acquisition record. A Human's actual Size determines relative-size clauses; borrowing never silently changes it.
2. **Survival choices offer different kinds of value.** Never Quite Finished gives +1 HP plus a death Boon; Hard as Bedrock proposes +2 HP with poor buoyancy; Bigger on the Inside gives +1 HP plus courage. Oath-Bound Will instead assists poison/disease checks. Compare their actual encounter frequency; poison rolls must not be invented merely to justify a trait. These are proposed useful niches, not proven equal mathematical values.
3. **Repeated selection must not multiply one trait.** Acquisition validation should preserve exactly two ancestry choices, one borrowed foreign trait in place of the Human choice, and no duplicate trait IDs. Resource totals apply each granted benefit once.
4. **Adaptable is the largest long-horizon concern.** It supplies +7 total DP by level 7, not merely a starting convenience. Test Human Adaptable alongside one borrowed high-value trait at levels 1, 3 and 7 against the proposed longer branch tails. Preserve its existing formula in this pass; do not silently trim it to compensate for another ancestry change.
5. **Speed stacking is intentional but must remain visible.** Wood Elf Grace plus Footfall yields +2 Speed in woods. Human can borrow one of these, not two. Distinct ancestry Speed benefits add; difficult terrain still follows each precise source.
6. **Cover and conditions use the universal rules.** Small Target's conditional extra defense Boon reverses to an attack Bane if the player attacks. It is not physical resistance. Frightened and deception benefits do not grant condition immunities. HP or carrying traits do not alter attribute scores.
7. **Repeated crafting bonuses can meet the dice cap, and repeated time costs need a single convention. If several traits demand twice ordinary task time, use the longest required time rather than multiplying those durations; distinct preparations (such as opening a mechanism) still take their printed time. This anti-multiplication convention is proposed, not currently established.** A trait may complement a paid Craft technique, but grants no recipe, tool proficiency substitute, extra action or free magical item. Gnome analysis and manipulation can both matter in a scene without both applying automatically to the same check.
8. **Rest and control are separate.** Waking reverie changes sleep's form; it does not grant work time, a shorter rest or extra recovery. Still Water no longer mentions concentration. Answering Voice applies the control spell's actual Reason permission and does not create a new universal control defense.
9. **Daily luck and death are deliberately connected.** A Little Luck works on a death roll; The Debt Comes Due cannot be paid at 0 HP. Never Quite Finished uses the ordinary Fortitude death procedure. No trait refreshes Grit or changes three death successes/failures.
10. **No trait duplicates a class signature's central action.** Improvements here mostly change circumstances and rolls. The new level-3/7 class developments should remain distinct sources of dramatic new capability. The twelve available starting branch choices remain the main active-choice load.
