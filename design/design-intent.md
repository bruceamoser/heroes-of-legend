# The experience we are building

**Agreed direction:** strategic character development, simple table play, familiar
fantasy with HOL’s own identity. Heroes should feel capable, distinctive and exciting.
The scars they carry make that capability matter.

## Start with the moment

A Protector gets between danger and a friend. A Blade moves through a fight with
purpose. An Arcanist changes what is possible. A Shepherd spends something of
themselves to bring another person back. An Intellect makes the discovery that
changes the plan. A Leader makes everyone else bolder. The Unbalanced turns the
battlefield into wreckage. A Shadow is where nobody expected. The Odd solves the
problem in a way that becomes a story afterward.

These are design promises, not restrictions on a player’s personality or purchases.
Class signatures express the promise; shared purchases let any class branch out.

## Review each option as a player

- Can I picture what my character does?
- Would I choose this because I want that moment, not merely because I need a tax paid?
- Does it give me a new decision or a satisfying expression of expertise?
- Does Weak still advance the scene, with Standard and Strong delivering more?
- Can another player help set this up or enjoy what it creates?
- Can we resolve it without stopping to negotiate a new subsystem?

Do not fill a tier with a bigger copy solely because the grid has an empty cell.
Higher tiers may affect more people, change an obstacle, rescue someone, or reshape
position. Large effects are welcome when their action, target and consequence are clear.

## HOL’s identity

Attacks connect. Consequences and outcomes matter. Equipment appearance is free;
a cleave does not become weaker because its owner calls the weapon a walking stick.
Spells are repeatable; healing costs the caster life. Displacement creates openings,
rescues and collisions. Free attacks reward teamwork without endless chains.
Permanent scars and finite Grit supply the dark edge beneath the heroics.

A character’s best ordinary trick should be useful now, not after three required
purchases. Higher-tier cards are independent purchases with discipline requirements,
not chains of obsolete abilities.

## What numerical review is for

Use arithmetic to find dead options, contradictory costs and combinations that
remove others’ turns. Do not flatten distinct abilities into equal expected damage.
A bridge, a rescue, a convincing disguise and a firestorm do different jobs.
Actual play must eventually answer whether each has a rewarding place.
